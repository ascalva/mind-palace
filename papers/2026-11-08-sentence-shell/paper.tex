\title{A Read-Only Shell for a Query Sentence Language,\\ with Completion Derived from Its Parser}
\author{Alberto Serrano-Calva}
\date{8 November 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
The palace answers queries written as sentences of a small typed language: verbs over notes,
joined by connectives, narrowed by adjectives, with a hypothetical modality. A language is learned
by writing it, and writing it is easier when the tool says what may come next. This article
describes \code{mind-palace shell}, an interactive shell over the language whose completion is
derived from the parser itself: a candidate token is offered exactly when the parser, reading the
prefix followed by that token, gets past it. Open argument slots are classified from the parser's
own refusal of the prefix and filled from the instance's catalog, its strata and the defaults in
force. The shell is read-only by construction: it calls only the compiler, the universe builder
and the executor, refuses requests of the action sort, takes no lock and writes no log. On the 167
compiling sentences of the language's test suite, every keyword, symbol, stratum, number slot and
free-text slot is offered at its position, and every note the catalog holds; completion takes a
median of under 0.5\,ms and at most 3\,ms with an empty catalog, and at most 14\,ms with 20{,}000
catalogued paths.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A query to the palace is a sentence, not a keyword list. The sentence
\code{near "sleep debt" and reach F from "health/sleep.md" depth 2} ranks notes by a phrase and
masks the ranking to what one note cites within two hops; the compiler checks that it is
well-formed, well-sorted and admissible under the asker's grant, normalizes it by proved
rewrites and runs it against a universe built for it. The language has twenty verbs and
connectives, four relation letters, a regular path language, fourteen strata and a hypothetical
modality. It is small, but its owner should not need to hold all of it in memory to write a query.

Two tools existed: \code{mind-palace query}, which runs one sentence, and the Ambassador's
conversation, where a model writes the sentence. Neither helps a person compose one. This change
adds a third: a shell for composing and running sentences, with completion, help, a plan view and
a one-page cheat sheet. The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item completion derived from the parser by probing it, with a proof that every keyword which can
  continue a prefix in the parser's language is offered (\cref{prop:complete});
\item slot filling from the instance: notes from the catalog and the reference index, strata from
  the type checker's own enumeration, corpus sources from the configuration, defaults from the
  precedents;
\item a shell that cannot write: its imports, its refusal of the action sort and its lock
  discipline are each checked by a test on a scratch instance;
\item a cheat sheet whose verb list is generated from the parser and pinned by a test.
\end{itemize}

\section{The language in brief}
The sentence algebra is described in the design record (\code{dn-query-sentence-algebra}); this
section states only what the shell relies on. A sentence denotes a value of one of five sorts: a
\emph{field} $f : V \to [0,1]$, a score per note; a \emph{region} $R \subseteq V$; a set of
\emph{chains}, paths in the typed graph; a \emph{witness}, the record that minted an edge; or a
\emph{text}, the content at a reference. The note set $V$ is the sentence's universe.

\paragraph{Verbs.} \code{near "t"} is the embedding kernel's column at a text.
\code{diffuse S from n} is the column of a diffusion kernel out of a note
\cite{kondor2002diffusion}. \code{reach F from n depth d} is the region reachable along a relation
letter: $S$ similarity, $F$ citation, $D$ version, $C$ causal. \code{walk from n via P} returns the
chains whose letter sequence lies in the regular language $P$ over $\{S,F,D,C\}$, found by a
product automaton \cite{mendelzon1995regular,hopcroft2006automata}. \code{relate}, \code{trace},
\code{witness} and \code{read} complete the clause verbs. \code{top k}, \code{atleast t},
\code{far} and \code{not} are operators on fields and regions.

\paragraph{Connectives and adjectives.} On fields, \code{and} is the pointwise (Schur) product and
\code{or} the convex mixture; on regions they are intersection and union; \code{and} of a field and
a region masks the field. \code{s then t} is \code{t within region(s)}. The adjectives
\code{within R}, \code{over strata} and \code{as of "cut"} bind tightest, so the grammar has four
levels: \code{then}, \code{or}, \code{and}/\code{minus}, and the adjectives. The composition laws
(masks commute with products, idempotence, the threshold passing a mask) are machine-checked in
\code{formal/Palace/Sentence.lean}, and the planner rewrites only by them, much as a relational
optimizer rewrites by the identities of the algebra \cite{codd1970relational}.

\paragraph{Hypotheses, precedents, the universe.} \code{assume h in s} evaluates $s$ on a
perturbed graph and answers as a \emph{Hyp} beside the actual answer, never as fact. Dials a
sentence leaves unsaid, such as the depth of \code{reach} or the strata of \code{near}, come from a
ledger of precedents. The universe is the sentence's \emph{support}: the strata its clauses read,
the nearest units to each text and anchor, the citation graph at the cut. What the universe cannot
answer is listed in its \code{missing} notes, which ride with every answer.

\section{The shell}

\subsection{Read-only by construction}
The shell compiles a sentence under the surfaces' grant, builds its universe from the instance
the configuration names, executes the plan, and prints the hits with their provenance labels and
the universe's notes. These three calls are the read path \code{mind-palace query} runs, without
its query log. The shell imports nothing from the ingress, the stores' writers or the scheduler;
it refuses a sentence of the action sort (\code{do \dots}) before any store is opened, because a
request lands through the daemon's single writer; it takes neither the supervisor lock nor a
store's writer lock; and a store held by its writer is reported as busy rather than waited on.
\code{plan s} stops before execution: it prints the normal form, each clause's scope, the seeds the
universe is built from, the support's size and the \code{missing} notes.

\subsection{Completion from the parser}
The parser is a recursive-descent parser \cite{wirth1996compiler} over a token stream, with one
token of lookahead and no backtracking: each method inspects the current token, consumes it or
raises an error naming the production and the token. Completion asks this parser, rather than a
second description of the grammar, what may come next.

\begin{definition}
Let $p$ be a token sequence and $k$ a token. Run the parser on $p\,k$. The parser \emph{consumes}
$k$ after $p$ if it accepts $p\,k$ or fails at a token index greater than $|p|$. The offered
keywords after $p$ are the keywords it consumes; the same test with one placeholder per token
class (a string, a number, a word that is no keyword) and with each symbol of the path syntax
decides which classes and symbols are offered.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:complete}
If $p\,k\,w$ is accepted by the parser for some token sequence $w$, then $k$ is offered after $p$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The parser's position only increases, and every decision it takes reads only the token at its
position. On the inputs $p\,k\,w$ and $p\,k$ the first $|p|+1$ tokens agree, so the two runs are
identical until the position first exceeds $|p|$. On $p\,k\,w$ it does exceed $|p|$, since the
whole input is accepted. So the run on $p\,k$ also reaches a position past $|p|$, after which it
either accepts or fails at an index greater than $|p|$.
\end{proof}

The converse holds wherever consuming a token commits the parser to a production that some
suffix completes, which is every production of this grammar but one value check (a diffusion's
$\beta > 0$, raised after the number is read); the only number the completer offers is the
precedent's default, which passes it. The test is the classical notion of a viable prefix \cite{aho2006compilers},
computed by running the parser instead of tabulating FIRST and FOLLOW sets.

\paragraph{Slots.} When the prefix is incomplete, the parser's refusal of it names what it
expected at the end: a note, a quoted string, a number, a path or a list of strata. The shell maps
that phrase to a slot. A note is filled with the paths and titles of the catalog and the endpoints
of the reference index, read through read-only SQLite connections, and with the
\code{corpus/<source>/} prefixes of the sources the configuration declares. A stratum is filled
with the strata the type checker accepts. A number is filled with the precedent the elaborator
would use (the default depth for \code{reach depth}, the default $k$ for \code{top}). When the
prefix is complete, a word placeholder is appended and the parse tree says whether it landed in
an \code{over} list or a path, the two places a complete sentence still takes a word. Inside a path
only the relation letters, the path symbols and the parser's stop words are offered.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Candidates for the word being typed.}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require a buffer $b$; the instance's catalog
\State $(p, f) \gets$ split $b$ into the prefix and the fragment being typed
\State $C \gets \{k \in \mathit{Keywords} : \textsc{Consumes}(p,k)\} \cup
       \{\sigma \in \mathit{Symbols} : \textsc{Consumes}(p,\sigma)\}$
\State $s \gets \textsc{Slot}(p)$ \Comment{from the parser's refusal of $p$, or the tree of $p$ plus a word}
\If{$s$ is a note} $C \gets C \cup$ catalog notes
\ElsIf{$s$ is strata} $C \gets C \cup$ strata
\ElsIf{$s$ is a number} $C \gets C \cup \{$the precedent's default$\}$
\ElsIf{$s$ is a path} keep only relation letters, stop words and symbols in $C$
\EndIf
\State \Return $\{c \in C : f \text{ is a prefix of } c\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Help, continuation and the cheat sheet}
The verbs the shell lists are those the parser consumes at the start of a sentence and after a
whole sentence, less \code{do}. Each has a help entry: its shapes, one example and what it
returns, with the precedents that apply. The entries are written once, and a test checks them
against the parser: every shape, expanded over its optional groups and alternatives, must parse;
every example must compile under the surfaces' grant; and every parser keyword must appear in some
shape. The cheat sheet's verb list is generated from the entries and the same test compares the
document to it, so a verb the grammar gains fails the suite until it is documented. Multi-line
composition uses the parser too: an entry continues on the next line while the parser's only
complaint is that the sentence ended early; an empty line ends it. The front end is the standard
library's readline binding \cite{pythonreadline,readline}, with a history file kept across
sessions.

\paragraph{The dependency.} The repository allows third-party libraries outside the sealed core,
and prompt\_toolkit \cite{prompttoolkit} would give richer completion menus. It is not used: it is
not in the locked dependency set, the runtime set is kept to libraries that cannot reach the
network and prompt\_toolkit ships a telnet server, and readline already provides completion,
history and line editing. The completer is a function from a buffer to candidates with no
dependency on the front end, so a different front end would replace about forty lines.

\section{Evaluation}

\paragraph{What the tests check.} On a scratch instance (every store path under a temporary
directory, one record landed in the catalog and the raw store): the completer offers every verb the
parser accepts, where the oracle is an independent trial of each keyword against ten argument
tails; every help shape parses and every example compiles; slots are filled from the instance;
\code{plan} completes with the executor replaced by a function that fails the test; an unknown verb
returns the parser's error with a caret under the word; and with \code{fcntl.flock} and the
supervisor lock's \code{acquire} replaced by functions that fail, running and planning sentences
and refusing two requests leave every store file's SHA-256 unchanged (SQLite's shared-memory
index aside) and create no query log and no spool.

\paragraph{Coverage.} The corpus is every string literal in \code{tests/unit/test\_sentence\_*.py}
that compiles without a grant and is not a request: 167 sentences, 1139 tokens. Each sentence is
replayed token by token, and a token counts as offered when the completer, given the prefix before
it, offers it (keywords, symbols, strata, notes) or recognizes its slot (a number, a free text, a
path's letters or count). \Cref{tab:coverage} gives the result with a catalog that holds the
corpus's notes and with an empty one. The first column is complete by construction for notes,
since the catalog was given them; it measures that the slot is recognized and filled. The empty
catalog shows what the grammar alone offers. At each position the completer offers a median of 5
candidates and at most 22, out of 37 keywords.

\paragraph{Latency.} The completer was called on every character prefix of the same sentences
(5582 calls) on an Apple M2 Pro under CPython 3.15, after a warm-up, in three runs. With an empty
catalog the median is 0.45 to 0.47\,ms, the 95th percentile 1.3\,ms and the maximum 2.4 to
2.9\,ms. With 20{,}000 catalogued paths the median is 0.69 to 0.70\,ms, the 95th percentile 9.7
to 9.9\,ms and the maximum 12 to 14\,ms; the added cost is the scan of the catalog at a note slot.
Each call runs about fifty parses of the prefix, so the cost grows linearly with the prefix's
length. The numbers are reproduced by \code{uv run python eval/shell\_completion.py}.

\paragraph{What is not measured.} The time a person takes to compose a sentence with and without
completion would need users and a protocol; it is not claimed here.

\section{Limitations}
Completion is exact for the parser's language, which is larger than the compiler's: the sort
checker, the scope check and the path compiler refuse some sentences the parser accepts, such as
\code{far} applied to a region, and the completer offers the tokens that lead there. Notes are
offered from the catalog and the reference index only; dialogue turns, paper references and
digests are not enumerated. Free text is not completed. Opening a fresh instance creates its
stores' empty schemas, as \code{mind-palace query} does; the no-write guarantee is for an instance
whose stores exist. The help entries are checked against the parser, not derived from it, so their
wording can lag the grammar's meaning even while their shapes cannot. Readline's completion
display is plain; there is no inline hint of a slot's kind.

\section{Future work}
Two extensions follow from the same oracle: offering only tokens after which the prefix still
typechecks, by running the sort checker on a completed probe; and enumerating dialogue and paper
references at a note slot under the strata in force.

\section{Conclusion}
A one-token-lookahead parser without backtracking already knows what may follow any prefix; asking
it is enough to drive completion, with a short proof that nothing it accepts is missed. With slots
filled from the instance and a read path that cannot write, the shell lets the language be learned
by writing it, and the cheat sheet stays in step with the grammar because a test regenerates it.
