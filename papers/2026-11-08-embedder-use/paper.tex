\title{How a Knowledge Store Uses Its Embedder:\\Six Choices Measured Across Domains}
\author{Alberto Serrano-Calva}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
An embedding model is the one component of a knowledge store that relates any text to any other
without rules written for its source. Whether the store uses it well is decided by choices around
the model, and we measure six of them in an open-source store against its live defaults, on two
public retrieval sets, 600 long Wikipedia articles and a corpus of 1.37 million chunks from six
sources, each comparison's arms, metric and falsifier committed before it ran and every arm paired
query by query with a sign-flip test. The lexical tokenizer normalized no Unicode, so one word in
two encodings was two terms and decomposed words split at their marks; NFKC with case folding
changes no ranking on these mostly English sets and is implemented, with a tokenizer identity that
every lexical store records, refuses to mix and re-derives under. The item's title in the document
template raises nDCG@10 by 0.029 and 0.038 on the public sets and costs nothing on the long
articles; dropping the title-only chunk the chunker emits beside single-paragraph abstracts gains
as much with no embedding at all. Late chunking helps short documents and loses recall on long
ones, and a control shows its gain on short documents comes from leaving the template's tokens out
of the mean. Task prompts gain nothing, 256 dimensions with rescoring lose nothing, larger chunk
budgets raise nDCG with half the vectors but leave the long articles' recall undecided, and
thresholds for semantic edges set per source from the data find fewer cross-source bridges per
false positive than one global threshold, whose bridges turn out to be carried by hub edges.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A knowledge store that holds text from many sources needs a way to relate an item to the others
that does not depend on where the item came from. Hand-written integrators relate items by rules
about one kind of event: a session log's tool calls, a commit's files. An embedding model
\citep{vera2025embeddinggemma} is the store's first integrator that is domain-free by
construction: it turns any text into a vector, and a cosine between vectors into an edge. Whether
the store uses that prior well is a set of engineering choices around the model rather than the
model itself, and this article measures six of them in an open-source store
\citep{serranocalva2026palace}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item the lexical tokenizer that sits beside the embedder, which normalized no Unicode;
\item the chunk budget, 1{,}200 characters, against budgets in characters and in the model's tokens;
\item whether a chunk is embedded with its document's context: the item's title in the document
  template, or late chunking \citep{gunther2024late};
\item the query's task prompt;
\item truncating the vector to fewer dimensions \citep{kusupati2022matryoshka};
\item how a semantic edge is thresholded: one global cosine, against thresholds set per source
  from the data, mutual nearest neighbours, and CSLS \citep{conneau2018word}.
\end{enumerate}

Two principles govern the study. A choice is judged across domains: two public retrieval sets,
SciFact \citep{wadden2020scifact} and NFCorpus \citep{boteva2016nfcorpus} from BEIR
\citep{thakur2021beir}, and long Wikipedia articles and a 1.37-million-chunk corpus of six open
sources. And a threshold must set itself from the data, a quantile or a held-out fit, never a
constant tuned for one source. Every comparison's arms, primary metric and falsifier were
committed before it ran. The contributions are the measurements, a fix to the tokenizer with an
identity that every lexical store records and refuses to mix, and a ranking of the remaining
changes by gain per cost.

\section{Background}

\paragraph{Lexical retrieval and normalization.} BM25 \citep{robertson2009bm25} scores a unit by
the query terms it shares, each weighted by its rarity; what a term is depends on the tokenizer.
Unicode gives one abstract text several encodings: a precomposed letter or a base letter with
combining marks (canonical equivalence), and ligatures, sub- and superscripts and full-width forms
that stand for plain characters (compatibility equivalence). NFC and NFKC map equivalent texts to
one form \citep{uax15}; case folding maps caseless-equal texts to one form. Stemming merges
inflections; Harman's S stemmer strips English plurals by three rules
\citep{harman1991suffixing}.

\paragraph{Chunks and their context.} A long document is cut into chunks that are embedded
separately, so a chunk's vector sees only its own text. Two remedies give it the document: a
document template that names the item's title (EmbeddingGemma's documented form is
\code{title: \{title\} | text: \{text\}}), and late chunking \citep{gunther2024late}, which embeds
the whole document once and averages each chunk's token embeddings, so every token has attended to
the rest of the document.

\paragraph{Prompts and dimensions.} EmbeddingGemma is trained with task prompts on the query side
(\code{task: search result}, \code{question answering}, \code{fact checking}) and with Matryoshka
representation learning, so a prefix of its vector is itself an embedding
\citep{vera2025embeddinggemma,kusupati2022matryoshka}.

\paragraph{Hubness.} In high dimensions a few points are among the nearest neighbours of many
others \citep{radovanovic2010hubs}; a threshold on raw cosine then gives a dense region of the
space many edges and a sparse one none. Mutual nearest neighbours keep an edge only when each end
is among the other's nearest; CSLS \citep{conneau2018word} scores a pair by twice its cosine less
each end's mean cosine to its own nearest neighbours.

\section{The store and the method}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{The store.} An item's text is cut by a block-aware chunker: paragraphs are packed
greedily up to 1{,}200 characters, a paragraph longer than the budget is cut into windows, and
each chunk after the first begins with the last 150 characters of the one before. Every chunk is
embedded by EmbeddingGemma-2 (Q8\_0, 768 dimensions), served by llama.cpp
\citep{gerganov2023llamacpp} with four slots of 2{,}048 tokens, under the document template
\code{title: none | text: \{text\}}; a query under \code{task: search result | query: \{text\}}.
The chunk vectors live in a plane at float16, content-addressed by the chunk's text; a document's
score for a query is its best chunk's cosine.

\paragraph{Data.} SciFact (5{,}183 abstracts, 300 test claims) and NFCorpus (3{,}633 documents,
323 test queries), each document written as an item \code{\{title\}\textbackslash n\textbackslash
n\{text\}}; W, 600 Wikipedia articles of the corpus instance (median 29~kB), the 442 that are the
gold of 543 questions whose source links them, and 158 drawn at random, with each article's title
as a second family of questions; and the corpus battery: 400{,}274 items in six sources, 1{,}369{,}944
chunks, 4{,}714 questions in 11 families (titles, citations, links between sources, and twin
records of one paper in two sources).

\paragraph{Metrics and the test.} recall@10 and nDCG@10 at the document level. Each arm is paired
with the live default query by query; the mean difference is tested by a sign-flip randomization
test with each query its own cluster (20{,}000 sign vectors; the interval is the test inverted)
\citep{good2005,phipson2010}, and Holm's adjustment \citep{holm1979simple} runs over an
experiment's arms. An arm is \emph{better} or \emph{worse} at a Holm-adjusted $p < 0.05$, and
\emph{no worse} when its interval's lower end is at or above $-0.01$.

\paragraph{The shared embedder.} The embedding server serves a working instance as well. The
study sent one stream of 64 texts a request, sampled the server's slots every five seconds through
every phase and probed them before every request, and reports costs in tokens embedded, which do
not depend on load; no timing is a result. Late chunking needs per-token vectors, so it ran on a
second server started for it with pooling off and stopped after it. With pooling off the server
returns one 768-dimension vector a token, and the mean over every token of an input equals the
pooled vector of the first server (cosine $1.0$, norms equal), so a late vector and an independent
vector differ only in the tokens pooled and the context they saw.

\section{The tokenizer and its identity}\label{sec:tokenizer}

The lexical index took maximal runs of letters and digits of the lower-cased text. Without
normalization one text in two encodings gave two sets of terms, and since a combining mark is not
a letter, a decomposed word split at its marks (\code{na\"{\i}ve} in NFD gave \code{nai} and
\code{ve}), and words of scripts whose vowel signs compose into no precomposed character lost
them (a Devanagari word became its consonants).

\begin{definition}
A tokenizer $\tau = (F, \phi, \mu)$ has a normal form $F \in \{\text{none}, \text{NFC},
\text{NFKC}\}$, a case fold $\phi \in \{\text{lower}, \text{casefold}\}$ and a flag $\mu$ for
combining marks. Its terms are $\tau(x) = R_\mu(F(\phi(F(x))))$, where $R_\mu$ takes the maximal
runs that start with a letter or digit and continue with letters and digits, and with combining
marks when $\mu$. Its identity is the tuple with the version of the Unicode database, whose
character classes and folds $R_\mu$ and $\phi$ read.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Invariance]\label{prop:invariance}
If $F$ is NFC (NFKC) and $x, y$ are canonically (compatibility) equivalent, then $\tau(x) =
\tau(y)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equivalent texts have one normal form, $F(x) = F(y)$ \citep{uax15}, and $\tau(x)$ depends on $x$
only through $F(x)$. The second normalization makes the result a function of $F(x)$ even when the
fold produces an unnormalized sequence.
\end{proof}

The legacy tokenizer is $(\text{none}, \text{lower}, \text{false})$ and is reproduced exactly; the
current one is $(\text{NFKC}, \text{casefold}, \text{true})$, chosen by the pre-declared rule of
\cref{sec:tok-results}. Only terms are normalized: chunk texts and content hashes stay on the raw
bytes, so no stored atom changes.

A query tokenized by one tokenizer and scored against units tokenized by another matches the
words the two agree on and misses the rest, and no score reveals it. The store therefore treats
the tokenizer as the vector plane treats its embedder:

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{The lexical store's identity (\code{core/stores/lexical.py}).}\label{alg:identity}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{Recorded}{}
  \State \Return the stored identity; else \code{LEGACY} if any version is held; else none
\EndFunction
\Function{Land}{versions} \Comment{one transaction}
  \State $r \gets$ \Call{Recorded}{}
  \If{$r$ is none} store $\tau$'s identity \ElsIf{$r \ne \tau$} refuse \EndIf
  \State write the units of each version, tokenized by $\tau$
\EndFunction
\Function{Score}{query}: refuse unless \Call{Recorded}{} $\in \{\text{none}, \tau\}$; tokenize by $\tau$
\EndFunction
\Function{Backfill}{} \Comment{\code{derive\_lexical}, run by the \code{embed} job first}
  \If{\Call{Recorded}{} $\notin \{\text{none}, \tau\}$} reset units, postings, versions and identity \EndIf
  \State land every active item the index has no record of, from the raw store
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[One tokenizer a store]\label{prop:one}
After any sequence of landings, resets and backfills, every unit a store holds was tokenized by
the tokenizer its identity names.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By induction on transactions. A reset removes every unit with the identity. A landing either finds
no identity and no version, so the store holds no unit and the landing records $\tau$ before
writing units by $\tau$; or finds $\tau$ and writes units by $\tau$; or finds another identity and
writes nothing. A store from before identities were recorded holds only legacy units and reads as
\code{LEGACY}. The units and the identity commit in one transaction, so no crash separates them.
\end{proof}

The currency reads (whether a version is held, how many units it has) do not depend on terms and
do not refuse, so a lazy landing's check for an unchanged item still answers, and a changed item
is refused rather than landed beside units of another tokenizer. The backfill's reset and its
landings run in the store's one writer, so nothing reads the emptied index between them.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

Every figure is a mean paired difference against the live default with its 95\% interval;
\emph{better}, \emph{worse} and \emph{no worse} are the decision words of \cref{sec:method}. The
study's record in the repository has every arm and family.

\subsection{Tokenization}\label{sec:tok-results}

The arms are the legacy tokenizer T0; NFC or NFKC with \code{lower} or \code{casefold} (T1 to
T4); T4 with combining marks inside a token (T5); and, for information, T5 with Harman's S stemmer
(T6). The rule implements the most normalizing arm, T5 first, that is not \emph{worse} than T0 on
any primary metric. No normalizing arm moves one: T1 to T5 rank every SciFact and NFCorpus query
exactly as T0 and give every corpus question the same recall@10 (0.874); the largest nDCG@10
difference on the corpus is $+0.003$ ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.69$). So T5 is implemented. The words it
merges are rare in these mostly English sets, 0.009\% to 0.028\% of token occurrences (7{,}211 of
the corpus's 1{,}053{,}985 terms merge into 3{,}237), and they are the forms a reader takes for one:
a ligature from PDF extraction, the micro sign and the Greek $\mu$, sub- and superscript digits
(H$_2$O$_2$, IC$_{50}$, m$^2$). Of the corpus's 1{,}338{,}585 atoms, 18{,}931 are not in NFKC and 24
would coincide with another under normalized hashes; hashes stay on the bytes. The stemmer raises
nDCG@10 on NFCorpus ($+0.014$, \emph{better}) and SciFact ($+0.012$) and lowers recall@10 on the
corpus battery ($-0.002$), whose questions are titles and names; it is English only and not
adopted. The corpus's stage-1 index had been built by an ASCII-only tokenizer, which loses
recall@10 against T0 ($-0.002$, \emph{worse}). The current tokenizer runs at 31 million characters a
second against the legacy one's 44.

\paragraph{The title-only chunk.} The chunker emits an item's title as a chunk of its own when the
first paragraph does not fit beside it, and carries all of it into the next chunk as its overlap.
Every multi-chunk document of SciFact and NFCorpus has one (29\% and 31\% of their chunks), and so
do 23\% of the corpus's OpenAlex chunks and 26\% of its arXiv chunks; 84{,}002 of its 1{,}369{,}944
chunks in all, almost none in Wikipedia, Stack Exchange, Gutenberg or Hacker News. Such a chunk is
mostly template, and its atoms are hubs: those of OpenAlex and arXiv have on average 19{,}242 and
15{,}779 atoms of other items above the cosine 0.785, twice the other atoms of the same sources.
Dropping it (Nd) gains as much as the title template, with no embedding.

\subsection{Task prompts and dimensions}

No task prompt is \emph{better} on the set whose task it names: question answering on NFCorpus
$+0.005$ (\emph{no worse}), fact checking on SciFact $-0.008$, and on NFCorpus $-0.011$
(\emph{worse}). The store keeps \code{search result}. For dimensions, the first 256 coordinates with
the 50 best chunks rescored by the full float16 vector are \emph{no worse} than 768 on SciFact
($+0.000$), NFCorpus ($-0.001$) and the corpus battery ($-0.000$) at a third of the resident
bytes; flat 256 loses on NFCorpus ($-0.014$, \emph{worse}), and 128 dimensions lose everywhere.

\section{Limitations}

Each figure is one run of one model, quantization and server; another embedder moves every
number, and the comparisons are paired within a run for that reason. The public sets are English
abstracts and the corpus is mostly English, so the Unicode fix shows its value in counts rather
than in recall: few terms change, and the retrieval figures cannot separate the arms. W is 600
articles of one source, sampled around the corpus battery's link questions, and its queries are
titles and links rather than judged relevance. The corpus battery's families are mostly lexical
by construction (titles, citations), which favours the lexical arm. The bridge reading depends on
the community partition and the null of stage 1, and an excess of bridges over the false-positive
bar is a property of the edge set as a whole, not a measure of any one edge. The embedder was
shared with a working instance and the late-chunking server shared the GPU, so no timing is
reported as a result; costs are in tokens. Gutenberg's books (108~MB) were left out of the
experiments that re-embed, for their cost.

\section{Future work}

The chunker's emission of a chunk that the next chunk carries whole is a domain-free defect with
a domain-free fix: do not emit a chunk wholly contained in the next one's overlap. The store's
lexical tokenizer treats a run of a script written without spaces as one term; a segmenter for
those scripts is the next consistency step, and so are joiners inside words (the zero-width
non-joiner of Persian). Thresholds for semantic edges that set themselves per source should be
tested against the store's own downstream readings (the walk, the diffusion, the rises), not only
the bridges. Late chunking with windows longer than the live server's slot needs a server
configured for it and a measurement of where the model's context stops helping.

\section{Conclusion}

The embedder was used well in its geometry (dimensions, threshold, prompt) and less well at its
input. The largest gains measured here come from what the store hands the model: a title that
names the document, and no chunk that is all template. Both are domain-free in form, and the
second is a defect of a domain-free rule that shows only on documents of one long paragraph. The
threshold for semantic edges needed no calibration at the item grain, and the bridges it produces
deserve a reading of their own before any threshold is tuned for them. The lexical index now
normalizes its terms, and a change of tokenizer can no longer mix two vocabularies in one index.
