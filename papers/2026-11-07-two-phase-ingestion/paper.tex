\title{Two-Phase Ingestion for a Verified Knowledge Store:\\
Completeness Without the Vector and a Convergent Embed Drain}
\author{Alberto Serrano-Calva}
\date{November 8, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
In a knowledge store queried over a graph of items, the embedding model produces exactly one of
the store's relations, semantic similarity; every other relation and nearly all of the mathematics
computed over them read the graph alone, and a tokenizer gives each item a lexical representation
with no model. The embedder is also the only slow stage of ingestion, 21 to 30 chunks a second on
the machine the store runs on. We separate ingestion into two phases: the landing writes an item's
bytes, label, explicit edges and lexical units and its idempotence key, so the item is complete
without its vector; a drain derives the vector afterwards, idempotently, converging from any
interleaving with landings and from any interruption, under the plane's own key. A query that
needs only structure or terms answers at once; one that needs a vector not yet derived reports
the item pending, ranked by the lexical index with a label, never guessed. Three laws state this
and are proved in Lean~4 over a finite model, with the natural mistakes as refuted controls: a key
written before its vector is never repaired, and a pending vector read as zero changes with the
embedding. The embedder, a prior learned elsewhere, thereby becomes a source whose contribution a
Shapley attribution over the lexical and semantic arms measures. On SciFact (\nItems{} abstracts,
\nQueries{} queries) landing without the embedder ran at \lazyRate{} items a second against
\syncRate{} with it, the drain derived vectors at \drainRate{} chunks a second with a median lag of
\lagMedian{}~s from key to vector, and the embedder's Shapley attribution at recall@10 was
\shapE{} against the lexical index's \shapL{}. The default instance keeps synchronous embedding;
one configuration key flips it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The store this article concerns ranks items by the kernel column of a similarity backbone
(\code{near}), diffuses along it, and reaches and walks along explicit relations: citations,
hyperlinks, a question to its answer, version chains, replies, references from code to code. The
walk sums and resolvents, the Hodge potential of a flow, the spectral gap and the $k$-cores of its
scale study read the graph and never a vector, and BM25 over an item's chunks
\citep{sparckjones1972,robertson2009bm25} needs no model. The embedder is the one stage of
ingestion a model must run, and on the store's machine it saturates near 30 chunks a second while
every other stage of landing an item takes milliseconds; for the hundred-gigabyte corpus the store
is built toward, that is sixty days of embedding against minutes of everything else. Ingestion
waited for the embedder before writing an item's key, so the slowest stage set the pace of the
whole.

We make ingestion two-phase. Phase~1 lands what the bytes and the source determine and writes the
idempotence key; phase~2 derives the vector in the background as a write into the vector plane
that is idempotent, converges from any interleaving and any interruption, and carries a key of
its own. The invariant is that an item is complete without its vector. The contributions are the
laws (\cref{sec:theory}): \ing{19}, a landing writes no vector and the landed item is complete,
and the drain changes neither keys nor structures and leaves every other embedder identity's field
alone, so re-embedding after a model change is the same drain under a new identity; \ing{20}, an
item's derivation is idempotent and commutes with another item's and with a later landing of
another item, the drain is idempotent and leaves nothing pending, the field converges from any
interleaving, the implemented drain over the pending items reaches it from sound truthful states,
and the key-first order is unsound and never repaired; \ing{21}, a structural query is invariant
under every embed run, a pending item's field is \lean{none}, a reported vector is the item's own,
and a pending vector read as zero changes with the embedding. All are proved in Lean~4
\citep{demoura2021,mathlib2020}, attacked beforehand by property testing with refuted controls
\citep{claessen2000}, and judged against their statements by a comparator. The design and its
implementation behind one configuration key follow (\cref{sec:design}), then measurements on a
public control set (\cref{sec:eval}). The design note of the repository carries the detail.

\paragraph{Related work.} Deferring a computation until needed and sharing the result is lazy
evaluation \citep{henderson1976lazy}; a derived index is a materialized view whose maintenance may
be deferred and must then be incremental and idempotent \citep{gupta1995views}, and dynamic text
indexes trade indexing time against query time by buffering and merging
\citep{buttcher2005tradeoffs,lester2006online}. The crash argument is write-ahead recovery's
\citep{mohan1992aries}: work before the key is redone without being repeated. Dense retrieval beat
BM25 on question answering while a hybrid beat either \citep{karpukhin2020dpr}; across
heterogeneous collections BM25 stays a strong baseline \citep{thakur2021beir}; reciprocal rank
fusion combines rankings robustly \citep{cormack2009rrf}. The information a source adds beyond
another is a conditional mutual information, $I(R; E \mid L)$, and the chain rule
$I(R; L, E) = I(R; L) + I(R; E \mid L)$ \citep{cover2006} is the telescoping an attribution needs;
the Shapley value is the unique efficient, symmetric, null-respecting, additive attribution over
sources \citep{shapley1953value}, sampled by orderings \citep{castro2009polynomial} and used to
value data \citep{ghorbani2019data}. The embedder is EmbeddingGemma \citep{vera2025embeddinggemma}.

\section{The invariant and the laws}\label{sec:theory}

\paragraph{The model.} Items $I$, structures $S$ (what a landing writes for an item: bytes, label,
edges and lexical units as one value, read only through functions of it), vectors $V$, embedder
identities $E$ (model, document template, dimension: the plane's fingerprint). A state is
\[
  s = (\mathrm{keyed} \subseteq I,\;
       \mathrm{struct} : I \to S_\bot,\;
       \mathrm{atom} : E \to S \to V_\bot,\;
       \mathrm{mark} : I \to E \to S_\bot):
\]
the items whose key is written, each item's landed structure, the plane's atom table per identity
(a structure's vector once embedded, content addressed, so the same bytes are embedded once and a
superseded version's atom stays as history), and each item's mark per identity, the plane's current
occupancy of the item, the structure whose rows the drain landed. The embedder is $f : E \to S \to V$.
A \emph{landing} of $i$ with $\sigma$ inserts $i$ into $\mathrm{keyed}$ and sets
$\mathrm{struct}(i) = \sigma$, writing no atom and moving no mark. For a keyed $i$ with structure
$\sigma$, an \emph{atom write} under $e$ sets $\mathrm{atom}(e, \sigma) = f(e, \sigma)$ and a
\emph{mark write} sets $\mathrm{mark}(i, e) = \mathrm{struct}(i)$; both do nothing for an unkeyed
item. An item's \emph{derivation} is its atom write then its mark write (\lean{derive}); the
\emph{key-first} order is the reverse (\lean{deriveKeyFirst}). A run applies a list of steps.
$i$ is \emph{complete} when keyed, \emph{pending} under $e$ when keyed and
$\mathrm{mark}(i, e) \neq \mathrm{struct}(i)$ (no vector yet, or an older version's), and its
\emph{field} under $e$ is $\mathrm{atom}(e, \mathrm{struct}(i))$ when keyed with
$\mathrm{mark}(i, e) = \mathrm{struct}(i)$ and \lean{none} otherwise: a derived item's vector, a
pending item reported. $\drain(f, e, s)$ gives every keyed item's structure its atom and every
keyed item its mark; the implemented drain, \lean{codeDrain}, does so for the pending items alone.
A state is \emph{sound} under $e$ when every derived item has the atom its mark promises, and
\emph{truthful} for $f$ when every atom is $f$ of its structure.

\begin{theorem}[\ing{19}; \lean{land\_atom}, \lean{land\_mark}, \lean{complete\_land},
\lean{structural\_invariant\_drain}, \lean{drain\_other\_atom}, \lean{drain\_other\_mark}]
A landing writes no atom and moves no mark, and the landed item is complete. For any $Q$ of the
keyed set and the structures, $Q(\drain(f, e, s)) = Q(s)$; and for $e' \neq e$, $\drain(f, e, s)$
has the atoms and marks of $s$ under $e'$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The drain is pointwise and leaves $\mathrm{keyed}$ and $\mathrm{struct}$ as they are; the rest
unfolds the conditionals on $e' = e$. Hence re-embedding after a model change is the same drain:
the plane forgets its identity on reset, every item is pending under the new one, and nothing of
another identity or of the landing is read or moved.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\ing{20}; \lean{derive\_idem}, \lean{derive\_comm}, \lean{derive\_land\_comm},
\lean{drain\_idem}, \lean{not\_pending\_drain}, \lean{field\_drain}]
A derivation run twice is the derivation run once; two items' derivations commute; for $i \neq j$
the derivation of $i$ commutes with a landing of $j$. The drain is idempotent, leaves nothing
pending, and gives a keyed $i$ the field $f(e, \mathrm{struct}(i))$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Written out, a derivation sets $\mathrm{atom}(e, \mathrm{struct}(i))$ and $\mathrm{mark}(i, e)$
and nothing else (\lean{run\_derive}); each equality is a case split on the conditionals, the
landing one using that a landing of $j \neq i$ leaves $\mathrm{struct}(i)$ fixed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\ing{20}, convergence; \lean{field\_drain\_run}, \lean{codeDrain\_field},
\lean{sound\_derive\_prefix}, \lean{truthful\_run}]
For any run $w$ of landings and embed steps, with $w|_{\mathrm{land}}$ its landings in order,
$\field(\drain(f, e, \mathrm{run}(w, s)), e) = \field(\drain(f, e, \mathrm{run}(w|_{\mathrm{land}}, s)), e)$.
From a sound and truthful state, the implemented drain has the drain's field; every prefix of the
key-last derivation keeps soundness, and every run keeps truthfulness.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Call two states agreeing when they share the keyed set and the structures. Every step's effect on
those two is a function of those two, so agreement is preserved under any step applied to both,
and an embed step leaves them as they were; by induction $\mathrm{run}(w, s)$ and
$\mathrm{run}(w|_{\mathrm{land}}, s)$ agree, and the field after a drain is a function of the two.
A derived item is skipped by the implemented drain; soundness gives it an atom and truthfulness
makes it $f(e, \sigma)$, the drain's value; a pending item is derived by both. The atom is written
before the mark, so when the mark names the structure the atom is there; only atom writes write
atoms, and they write $f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\ing{20}, the controls; \lean{mark\_first\_unsound},
\lean{mark\_first\_does\_not\_converge}]
If $i$ is keyed with structure $\sigma$ and $\mathrm{atom}(e, \sigma)$ is \lean{none}, the
key-first order interrupted after its mark leaves an unsound state, and when no other pending item
shares $\sigma$ the implemented drain of that state gives $i$ the field \lean{none} where the drain
gives $f(e, \sigma)$: the item is never repaired.
\end{theorem}

On the implementation this is the plane's order (PLN-4): atoms are inserted before the occupancy
transaction, so a drain that dies between them leaves the item pending and the atoms held, and
the next drain embeds nothing (\ing{5}) and lands the rows; the test that makes the transaction
die once checks exactly that.

\begin{theorem}[\ing{21}; \lean{structural\_invariant}, \lean{pending\_field\_none},
\lean{field\_some\_truthful}, \lean{zeroRead\_changes}]
For any $Q$ of the keyed set and the structures and any run $w$ of embed steps,
$Q(\mathrm{run}(w, s)) = Q(s)$. A pending item's field is \lean{none}; on a truthful state,
$\field(s, e, i) = v$ implies $\mathrm{struct}(i) = \sigma$ and $v = f(e, \sigma)$. The control:
reading $\mathrm{atom}(e, \mathrm{struct}(i))$ with $0$ for a missing one and no mark consulted
gives, for a keyed $i$ with no atom and a nonzero score, a value that changes across
$\mathrm{derive}(e, i)$: an answer about the embedding's progress, not the item.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Checking.} The 22 statements are restated in a specification module with their proofs
left open and judged identical to the proved ones by a comparator, so no proof file can change
what is proved. Before the proofs, each law with an executable instance was attacked on exact
shadows (two items, structures, vectors and identities; states and embedders from codes; runs from
lists of codes): fourteen attacks survived, twelve on all 500 instances and the two guarded ones on
486 and 467, and five controls were refuted, a run with a landing in it within 45 instances, a
landing of the same item not commuting with its derivation on the first, the key-first order
unsound within 17 and unrepaired within 6, and the zero reading within 8. The library builds with
warnings as errors, the sorry budget is zero, and the export replays through six kernel checkers.

\section{Design}\label{sec:design}

\paragraph{Phase 1, the writer.} Under \code{[embedding] lazy = true} a store effect lands its
item's raw object, parses and chunks it as before, lands the chunks' lexical units, the version
row where the place keeps chains, the attestation and, last, the catalog's digest, the idempotence
key (\ing{7}); it embeds nothing and writes no plane row. The UNCHANGED check, which asked the
plane whether it holds the version's rows current, asks the lexical index instead, since the rows
are now the drain's and their absence is pending, not unlanded: an active key implies the units
are held, \ing{7}'s invariant at the item's grain. A plan whose landing left any item without its
vector asks for the drain once, as a coalescing job folded into the row already waiting; it is
charged its writes and no embeds. Off, the writer is byte for byte what it was.

\paragraph{The lexical index.} The units are the chunks the same chunker derives from the raw
bytes, so lexical and vector rankings rank the same things. A unit's row keeps its path, version,
position, class, title and token count; a posting keeps a term's count in a unit; tokens are runs
of letters or digits in any script, lower-cased. Landing is idempotent by reconciliation as the
plane's is: a version's units are written once, then made current, and the path's other versions
stop being current, the currency flag the transaction's last write; a retire makes the units not
current and keeps them. A query is scored over the current units of the classes a reader may see
with BM25, $k_1 = 1.2$, $b = 0.75$ \citep{robertson2009bm25}, the document frequencies, the unit
count and the mean length read at query time, so a landing changes no stored weight. The index
also records each version it lands with its count of units, in the same transaction, so an item
with no text (recorded with none) is told apart from one landed before the index was opened (no
record): the first is complete, the second pending. Reading a missing unit as an empty item would
leave every item of an earlier landing without a vector forever. A batched landing of fresh
stores lands every store's units in one transaction, before any key.

\paragraph{Phase 2, the drain.} \Cref{alg:drain} is one run. It is a job of the one writer over
the writer's own stores; the housekeeping and the catch-up enqueue it when the key is on, the
command line runs it on demand, and \code{do sync\_embed} is its script. Re-embedding after a
model change resets the plane, which forgets the atoms, the occupancies and the identity, and runs
the same drain; the catalog, the raw store, the versions, the edges and the lexical index are
untouched. The server refuses an input longer than its batch of 2{,}048 tokens rather than
truncating it, and 1{,}200 characters do not bound the tokens (one comment of 1{,}145 characters
took 2{,}951), so the drain splits a refused group in halves until the text is alone and cuts the
text to the longest prefix the server takes, as the model would truncate it; any other failure is
raised. The lexical index is derived as the vectors are: a backfill lands the units of every item it
has no record of, from the raw store, and run again writes nothing.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{The embed drain (\code{core/ingest/derive.py}), batch $B$, streams $m$.}
\label{alg:drain}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $P \gets$ active catalog entries of a plane class whose $(\mathit{path}, \mathit{digest})$ the plane does not hold current, less those the index records with no unit \Comment{the plane read once}
\State $G \gets \emptyset$
\ForAll{$e \in P$}
  \State $r \gets$ the bytes of $e$ by digest, parsed and chunked as the landing chunked them
  \If{$r$ has a chunk} add $(r, \text{distinct chunks of } r)$ to $G$ \EndIf
  \If{$G$ holds at least $B \cdot m$ chunks} \Call{Flush}{$G$} \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Call{Flush}{$G$}
\Procedure{Flush}{$G$}
  \State embed the distinct chunk texts of $G$ whose atoms the plane lacks, $B$ a request, $m$ in flight
  \State land the rows of $G$ in one plane transaction \Comment{atoms first, then the occupancies: the key, last}
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Query semantics.} An item landed without a vector, or whose plane rows are an older
version's than the catalog's, is a node of the universe without a vector and a member of its
pending set; its row of the vector matrix is zero, so the kernel column scores it 0, which is no
score, and the backbone has no edge at it. \code{near} gives the pending nodes of its clause the
lexical index's score for the text, each path's best unit divided by the best among them, so the
best pending match scores 1 on a scale of its own; every such hit is labelled
\code{ranked\_by: lexical: the vector is pending}, and the answer carries a note with the count and
that the two parts are not one scale. Without an index the pending nodes score nothing and the
note says so. The universe's report names the pending items; structural verbs read nothing the
drain writes; the float16 margin certificate of a ranked read is computed over the vectors the
store holds, which excludes every pending item.

\paragraph{The embedder as a source.} With $v(\emptyset) = 0$, $v(\{L\})$ the lexical arm's score
on a battery, $v(\{E\})$ the semantic arm's and $v(\{L, E\})$ the hybrid's (reciprocal rank fusion),
the embedder's Shapley value is
$\varphi_E = \tfrac12\bigl(v(\{E\}) - v(\emptyset)\bigr) + \tfrac12\bigl(v(\{L, E\}) - v(\{L\})\bigr)$
and its leave-one-out reading is $v(\{L, E\}) - v(\{L\})$, the estimand of the attribution loop's
sign-flip test. The loop records an arm by the digest of its configuration, which names the sources
it may use; the embedder joins that set, and both readings are functions of the ledger.

\section{Measurements}\label{sec:eval}

The harness (\code{eval/two\_phase\_bench.py}) builds a scratch palace in a temporary root with its
own vault, stores and lexical index and the stack's embedder, EmbeddingGemma at 768 dimensions
served by llama-server on the loopback, and touches no live store. The control set is SciFact
\citep{wadden2020scifact} as distributed in BEIR \citep{thakur2021beir}: \nItems{} abstracts, each
a markdown item of its title and text cut at 1{,}200 characters so that every item is one chunk,
and the \nQueries{} test claims with graded relevance. The items land in ten requests of the
ingress language, lazily and synchronously, on separate scratch palaces. A timing is only as good
as the machine it ran on, so the harness samples the embedder's busy slots every five seconds
through each phase, with the load average at the phase's start and end. The timings below are a
control run in the order lazy, drain, synchronous, lazy again, with no other caller of the
embedder (no slot sample showed one); the two lazy runs, one before and one after, differed by
\lazyDrift\%. A first run, made while another ingest's backfill
embedded on the same server throughout, landed synchronously at \firstSyncRate{} items a second
(a ratio of \firstRatio$\times$) and drained at \firstDrainRate{} chunks a second; it is kept in
the record and not used here. The retrieval figures do not depend on timing and are the
first run's. Every figure is one run, read to one significant figure.

\paragraph{Landing, the drain, the lag.} Without the embedder the ten requests landed the \nItems{}
items at \lazyRate{} items a second; with it, at \syncRate{}, a ratio of \speedup$\times$ in landing
time. Both slow as the store grows: a lazy request took \planFirst{}~s first and \planLast{}~s
last, the attestation lookup's pass over the ledger, and a synchronous one \syncPlanFirst{}~s and
\syncPlanLast{}~s, where each item's plane transaction is its own. The drain derived the \nItems{}
chunks at \drainRate{} chunks a second on one stream at batch 64, a group of 64 committed at a
time, which is the embedder's measured rate. With every item landed and the drain started at
once, the lag from an item's key to its vector being current was \lagMedian{}~s at the median,
\lagNinety{}~s at the 90th percentile and \lagMax{}~s at most: the landing's time plus the
item's position in the drain's queue, which a steady arrival spreads evenly. A second drain found
nothing pending and embedded nothing.

\section{Limitations}

The model takes an item's structure as one value and says nothing about the landing's own stages
(\ing{7}'s model does), the plane's cache and search (PLN-2 to PLN-4) or the lexical arithmetic;
it assumes a deterministic embedder, which content addressing makes harmless for idempotence but
not for the statement that an atom is $f(e, \sigma)$. The lexical index is SQLite postings with
frequencies read at query time, right for a few hundred thousand units and not for a hundred
gigabytes; a bulk-built sparse matrix, which the corpus measurements already load, or an on-disk
inverted index is the next form for serving queries. The daemon's drain does not yield to other
callers of the embedder; the corpus runner's backfill does, through its gated embedder. The
fallback's scale is a design choice, labelled rather than calibrated. The battery is one
collection, items cut at one chunk, each figure one run; it measures the sources on SciFact's
claims, not on the store's own questions, which the attribution loop is for. Yielding, parallel dispatch of the drain beside the
writer (which \ing{20}'s commutation licenses), two identities at once for a migration, and the
loop on the corpus instance are the next steps.

\section{Conclusion}

An item is complete without its vector: landing writes what the bytes and the source determine,
and the one learned relation is derived afterwards by a drain that is idempotent and converges
from any interleaving and any interruption under a key of its own, written last. Queries that need
the graph or the terms answer at once; queries that need a vector not yet derived say so and never
guess. The laws are proved, attacked and judged, the code transcribes them behind one configuration
key that is off by default, and the embedder has a measured attribution as a source: on SciFact,
\shapE{} against the lexical index's \shapL{} at recall@10.
