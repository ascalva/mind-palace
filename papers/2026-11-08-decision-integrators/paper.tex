\title{Decision Models as Generic Integrators:\\ Typed Meaning beside the Embedding}
\author{Alberto Serrano-Calva}
\date{8 November 2026}

\begin{abstract}
An embedding model is a generic integrator: it gives every text a vector and every pair a
similarity, but a similarity cannot say whether a passage supports a claim or contradicts it. A
decision model answers typed questions about supplied state with probabilities, and with its
questions held as declared, versioned data it can attach typed meaning to any node or edge. This
article designs the layer that holds such answers beside the vector (fields keyed by the item, the
integrator's identity and the question set's digest, derived by a drain after landing), proposes
six laws that generalize those of a lazily embedded vector, and adds a question-discovery loop
whose four guard rails are laws: held-out keeping, bounded questions, attributable versions, and
no weight without measured gain. On public data, with arms and falsifiers declared before the runs,
cosine similarity separates supporting from contradicting abstracts no better than chance (AUROC
0.536) while a hosted decision model does (0.974, macro-F1 0.835, calibration error 0.078); judging
the embedding's shortlist lifts contradiction mining from 0.155 to 0.465 average precision,
question-to-answer linking from 0.905 to 0.981 MRR, and NFCorpus nDCG@10 by 0.047. A local
0.8-billion-parameter decision model carries signal (AUROC 0.854 on polarity) but clears the
declared bar for no family. Reading individual answers showed three benchmark labels measuring a
relation other than the one asked.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A store that builds context from many sources needs integrators: procedures that turn any raw
input into structure. Some are tailor-made. A transcript integrator that turns session logs and
commits into causal edges by rules written for those events produces good structure for those
events and nothing for any other source. A generic integrator applies to any text. The embedding
model is the standard one: every chunk gets a vector, every pair of texts a similarity, and the
store a semantic backbone over which retrieval, clustering and the graph's spectral measures run
\citep{reimers2019sbert}. A similarity is one number with one meaning. It cannot say whether a
passage supports a claim, contradicts it, or shares its vocabulary and says nothing about it; those
are the relations natural-language inference was built to label \citep{bowman2015snli}, and the
ones a store needs to find contradictions, revisions and answers.

A decision model answers typed questions about supplied state with probabilities: a Choice among
named options, a Noul (the probability that a statement is true), a Score on described levels
\citep{typesafedocs}. Held as declared, versioned data, generic questions turn such a model into
a second generic integrator: it can attach a kind to any node and a polarity to any edge. This
article asks whether that integrator adds meaning the embedding cannot give, whether a small local
model is good enough to be its default, and how its answers should live in a store. The
contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a layer for typed derived fields beside the vector, keyed by the item, the integrator's
  identity and the question set's digest, computed by a drain after landing, with six proposed
  laws that generalize the laws of a lazily embedded vector to several derived fields
  (\cref{sec:layer});
\item a question-discovery loop with four guard rails stated as laws: questions kept only on
  held-out rows, bounded in number and budget, versioned and attributable, and never raised in
  weight without measured gain (\cref{sec:discovery});
\item measurements on public data with ground truth, with arms and falsifiers declared before the
  runs, of a 0.8-billion-parameter local decision model and a hosted one against an embedding
  model, on polarity, the two integrators composed for re-ranking, contradiction mining, subject,
  kind, reply and equivalence, and a discovery pilot (\cref{sec:evaluation}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and related work}
\paragraph{Similarity and its blind spot.} Dense retrieval embeds queries and documents so that
relevant pairs are near \citep{reimers2019sbert}, and benchmarks such as BEIR measure it across
collections \citep{thakur2021beir}. Nearness is symmetric in polarity: a finding and its negation
share their terms and their topic. SciFact pairs scientific claims with abstracts that support or
contradict them, and with cited abstracts that do neither \citep{wadden2020scifact}; it is the
polarity task in its plainest form.

\paragraph{Judging candidates.} Cross-encoders that read a query and a candidate together re-rank
a retriever's shortlist far better than the retriever orders it \citep{nogueira2019passage}; the
decision model here plays that role with a typed question in place of a trained relevance head.
Reciprocal rank fusion combines two orders without calibrating their scores against each other
\citep{cormack2009rrf}. nDCG measures a ranking with graded relevance \citep{jarvelin2002cumulated}.

\paragraph{Calibration.} A probability is useful to code only if it means what it says. The Brier
score \citep{brier1950} and the log loss are proper scores; expected calibration error bins the
predicted probability and compares each bin's mean with its observed frequency
\citep{naeini2015ece,guo2017calibration}; AUROC measures ranking alone \citep{hanley1982auc}.
Intervals here are percentile bootstraps \citep{efron1979bootstrap} over the natural clusters.

\paragraph{Attribution and signed graphs.} The Shapley value is the unique efficient, symmetric,
null-respecting and additive attribution of a value over coalitions \citep{shapley1953}; the
palace's attribution law (SCL-3) applies it to the sources a configuration switches on. A signed
graph is balanced when its vertices split into camps with every negative edge between camps
\citep{harary1953balance}; the palace computes balance and frustrated triangles from its signed
edges, which today come from authored properties and a large chat model's one-word labels.

\section{Typed derived fields}\label{sec:layer}
\begin{definition}[Integrator]
An \emph{integrator} is a pair $J = (m, Q)$ of a model identity $m$ (a versioned model id, or a
local model's name and weights digest) and a question set $Q$, a finite map from question ids to
typed questions. Its \emph{digest} $\delta(Q)$ is a hash of $Q$ with the ids sorted and each
question's own order kept, since the order of a Choice's options is part of what a model reads.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Field]
For an item $i$ with bytes $b_i$, a \emph{node field} of $J$ is a partial map
$F_J : (h(b_i), m, \delta(Q)) \mapsto a$, where $h$ is the content hash and $a$ the whole typed
answer: a Choice's probability vector, a Noul's probability, a Score's level probabilities. An
\emph{edge field} keys an ordered pair $(h(b_i), h(b_j))$ the same way. A key without an answer is
\emph{pending}.
\end{definition}

An answer is never edited: a new model or a new wording is a new key, and the old answers stay
keyed by the old identity, as the vector plane keeps one atom per chunk text and embedder. The
thresholds that turn a probability into an action live in code, and the band between them is a
state of its own. Answers are derived after landing by a drain of the store's one writer, exactly
as the vector is under two-phase ingestion: landing never waits, and a query that needs a missing
answer says so.

\Cref{tab:laws} states six laws for the layer. INT-1 to INT-3 generalize three laws already
proved for one derived field, the vector (completeness without it, convergence of its drain, and
the soundness of pending reports), from one embedder to a set of integrators; INT-4 makes identity
explicit; INT-5 keeps hosted integrators on the network side of the store's boundary; INT-6 bounds
an answer's influence on rankings and keeps authored structure out of its reach. They are proposed
with falsifiers, not yet proved.

\paragraph{Where answers go.} The polarity family is a producer for the store's signed edges: a
decided label whose probability clears an upper threshold becomes an edge with provenance
\emph{interpreted} and weight $\cos \times p$, so balance, frustrated triangles and the camps of a
hypothetical edge read it unchanged. A supersession candidate's score
$s(C, D) = \mathrm{sim}(C, D)\, \mathbf{1}[\mathrm{contradiction}]\, \mathbf{1}[t(D) > t(C)]$ takes
$P(\mathrm{contradicts} \mid C, D)$ in place of the indicator. A kind field scopes a query's
carrier. A relevance or reply field re-ranks the embedding's shortlist on a scale of its own.

\section{Question discovery}\label{sec:discovery}
The integrators answer declared questions. Above them, a loop decides what to ask: it proposes
candidate questions from the generic families (or a local chat model drafts them), measures what
each adds beyond the store's current fields on held-out rows, keeps the ones that pay and retires
the rest, following the feature-discovery pattern in which a proposer writes questions, a decision
model answers them for every row and a classical model trained on the answers judges each change
on rows the proposer never read \citep{typesafedocs}.

\paragraph{What a question is worth.} Let $Y$ be a label, $X$ the current features and $A_q$ the
answers to question $q$. For the Bayes-optimal predictors, the expected log loss with and without
$A_q$ differ by the conditional mutual information:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[-\log p(Y \mid X)] - \mathbb{E}[-\log p(Y \mid X, A_q)] = H(Y \mid X) - H(Y \mid X, A_q)
= I(Y; A_q \mid X).
\end{equation}
With fitted models, the drop in held-out log loss estimates it, biased down by
misspecification. With the value $v(S) = \mathrm{LL}(\varnothing) - \mathrm{LL}(S)$ of a set of
sources, the Shapley value splits $I(Y; X_S)$ among the sources, the embedding among them.

\paragraph{The guard rails.} DSC-1: a question is kept only for its gain on rows disjoint from the
rows it was drafted from and the rows its scoring model was fit on, and the series is read once on
a split no pass read; this is the defence against wording tuned to the cases it was written from.
DSC-2: at most $K$ questions an item and a declared budget a pass. DSC-3: every kept question has
an id, a version, a family and a digest, with its measured gain and Shapley share recorded under
the digest. DSC-4, borrowed from a bounded utility field: a question's weight moves by
\begin{equation}
w \leftarrow w + \lambda (b - w) + \mathrm{clamp}(g, -c, c),
\end{equation}
with $g$ the held-out gain measured in the pass and $0$ when none was measured. Without measured
gain the weight only decays toward $b$; it stays in $[b - c/\lambda, b + c/\lambda]$; and a
question whose weight falls under a declared line retires.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{One pass of question discovery (greedy forward selection).}\label{alg:select}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require base features $X$, candidates $\{A_q\}$, labels $y$, disjoint fit and check rows
\State $S \gets \varnothing$; $\ell \gets \mathrm{LL}_{\mathrm{check}}(\mathrm{fit}(X))$
\While{$|S| < K_{\max}$}
  \State $q^\star \gets \arg\min_{q \notin S} \mathrm{LL}_{\mathrm{check}}(\mathrm{fit}(X, A_S, A_q))$
  \If{$\ell - \mathrm{LL}_{\mathrm{check}}(\cdot) < g_{\min}$} \State \textbf{break} \EndIf
  \State $S \gets S \cup \{q^\star\}$; update $\ell$
\EndWhile
\State \Return $S$, scored once on the held-out split
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Implementation}
The harness (\code{eval/decision\_integrators.py}) builds one request per item or pair: the
state is the text in named fields (a claim and a passage with its title, a document, a post), and
the questions are a declared family from one module (\code{eval/decision\_questions.py}). Every
request and its answer are kept, keyed by the hash of the payload, so a rerun asks nothing twice
and any result can be asked again. The local arm posts to the decision model's endpoint on the
loopback; the hosted arm writes its requests to a file and runs the edge's own client as a
separate process that reads its key from the system keychain, prices each answer's input tokens
into a ledger and stops at a dollar limit, so the process that reads the store never holds a
network connection. A failed request is an error row, counted and never read as an answer. The
embedding model is the store's own, one stream, and its vectors are cached.

The polarity family follows a published citation-check pattern: a Choice ``How does the passage
relate to the claim?'' over \emph{supports}, \emph{contradicts} and \emph{says nothing}, its
reverse as an order control, and three Nouls: supports, contradicts, and a companion asking whether
the passage reports a finding on what the claim asserts. The Noul variant combines two
independent judgments $s$ and $c$ into
\begin{equation}
p \propto \bigl(s(1-c),\; c(1-s),\; (1-s)(1-c)\bigr),
\end{equation}
renormalizing away the impossible ``both''.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:evaluation}
All figures come from the record \code{docs/experiments/decision-integrators-2026-11-08.md}
in the repository \citep{serranocalva2026palace}, which fixed the arms, ten falsifiers, three
controls and a default bar before any run. The local model is \code{tev1:0.8b} (752 million
parameters, served by Ollama under a tag with an 8192-token window); the hosted model is
\code{jev-1.14.0}; the embedding model is EmbeddingGemma-2 on the store's own server, in the formats
an earlier bench measured. The data: SciFact's original release, every labelled pair of train and
dev \citep{wadden2020scifact}; BEIR SciFact and NFCorpus for re-ranking
\citep{thakur2021beir,boteva2016nfcorpus}; and from a corpus instance of the store, 780 arXiv items
over 13 archives, 600 Stack Exchange posts, 300 questions with a pool of 889 answers, and 400
question pairs. The hosted arm sent only public text and spent \$0.74.

\paragraph{Polarity.} \Cref{tab:results} carries the first answer. The cosine separates supporting
from contradicting abstracts at AUROC 0.536 (95\% interval [0.424, 0.638]): similarity is blind to
polarity, as the premise said. The hosted Choice reaches macro-F1 0.835 and exceeds the embedding's
logistic arm by [0.459, 0.574]; its probabilities are calibrated (ECE 0.078), the three-Noul rule
does slightly better (0.864, ECE 0.049), and reversing the options changes 2.6\% of answers. The
local model's answers carry the signal (AUROC 0.854) but its Choice puts its top mass on
\emph{supports} for 298 of 339 pairs (macro-F1 0.386, ECE 0.354); its interval against the embedding,
[$-0.019$, 0.148], reaches zero, so the declared falsifier fires for it.

\paragraph{Composition.} The embedding's top 20 holds all 71 labelled contradictions of SciFact's
dev claims among 6{,}000 pairs, and cannot say which they are: ranked by cosine, 22\% of the top 50
are contradictions; ranked by the hosted model's $P(\mathrm{contradicts})$, 42\%, with average
precision 0.155 to 0.465 (a lower bound, since uncited abstracts are unlabelled). Judging each of
the ten nearest answers lifts question-to-answer MRR@10 from 0.905 to 0.981 ([0.049, 0.105]); judging
NFCorpus's top 20 with ``Does the passage contain an answer to the query?'' lifts nDCG@10 by 0.047
([0.033, 0.061]). On SciFact it does not ([$-0.049$, 0.013]). The answers say why: BEIR marks a
claim's cited abstract relevant even when the release finds no evidence in it, for 112 of the 300
claims, and the question asked whether a passage decides the claim. On the 188 claims with
evidence the judged order scores 0.940 against 0.920; on the 112 without, 0.646 against 0.731. The
local model's judgments lower every ranking (SciFact $-0.21$, NFCorpus $-0.09$, linking $-0.19$),
and fusion halves its losses without removing them.

\paragraph{Subject, kind and equivalence.} A zero-shot Choice over 13 archive names matches a $k$NN
that needs 779 labelled neighbours (0.526 against 0.558, interval reaching zero); 555 of the items
are cross-listed, and half of the hosted model's errors name an archive the item is listed in.
Whether a post asks a question is answered almost perfectly by the hosted model (AUROC 0.996; the
kind Choice's ECE 0.011), above the question-mark rule (0.848) and far above $k$NN (0.685); the
local model orders posts well (0.898) and places nearly all of them above one half. Duplicate
questions were paired with each one's nearest unlinked neighbour, so the cosine is near chance by
construction (0.504) and the hosted model ranks them at 0.869; 67 of the 200 duplicate pairs
involve one of eight canonical posts that specific questions are closed against, which the model,
asked whether two posts ask the same question, reads literally as different (median 0.07); without
them its AUROC is 0.935.

\paragraph{Discovery.} On SciFact the hosted pilot kept the polarity Choice and three edge questions
(mentions the claim's main entities; mentions its subject only in passing; reports an effect in the
opposite direction) and refused the fifth candidate at a gain of 0.0048 nats; held out, the kept set
lowers log loss from the embedding's 1.075 to 0.399 (gain interval [0.569, 0.786] nats), and the
embedding's Shapley share is $-0.007$ nats against the Choice's 0.293. The local pilot kept six,
the cap, with the two Scores carrying what its Choice misses (1.075 to 0.772). Questions about the
claim alone (hedged, time-bound, causal) added almost nothing to polarity.

\paragraph{Controls and the bar.} The 8192-token tag answered as the original tag (60 of 60 top
answers, median difference 0.004); both models repeated themselves (60 of 60 and 59 of 60); the
embedding reproduced its bench (0.849, 0.368). The declared bar asked a local family to beat the
embedding with an interval above zero, to have ECE at most 0.10, and to reach 0.8 of the hosted
model. The local model clears it for no family; for kind it passes the first and third tests and
fails calibration, which makes it a ranker. The hosted model clears the first two for polarity,
kind, reply and answer-style relevance. Its median request took 0.10\,s with the network; the local
polarity request took 0.72\,s on one stream with the embedding server busy.

\paragraph{The labels are not the questions.} Three labels measured a relation other than the one
asked: arXiv's primary archive against cross-lists, BEIR's citation against evidence, and a site's
closed-as-duplicate against asking the same question. Each was found by reading individual answers,
and in each the typed question answered what it asked. This is the case for measuring a family on
more than one source before it ships.

\section{Limitations}
The polarity and re-ranking results rest on two biomedical collections and the kind,
reply and equivalence results on three Stack Exchange sites; each family should be measured on a
second source before it is called generic. SciFact labels only cited abstracts, so contradiction
precision is a lower bound and some ``says nothing'' labels are debatable. The embedding server was
shared with another study for the whole run, so no embedding timing is claimed and local latency
was measured under contention; only the polarity run was single-stream. Three readings (cross-lists,
canonical posts, evidence) were added after reading answers and are labelled post hoc. The laws are
proposed with falsifiers and not yet proved. One pass of discovery cannot exercise the weight rule,
which needs several.

\section{Future work}
Measure tev1's 4-billion-parameter sibling against the same bar on the same benchmarks; a
monotone recalibration fit on held-out rows, recorded as part of the integrator's identity, may
rescue the small model's probabilities where its order is good. Measure the ``covered by'' variant
of equivalence and the \emph{elaborates} option of polarity on data that labels them. Prove the
layer's laws on the model already used for the lazily embedded vector, with attacks and controls.
Run the discovery loop for several passes, with a proposer that reads only its read rows' misses,
to exercise the weight rule and to measure what passes after the first one add.

\section{Conclusion}
A decision model with declared, versioned questions is a second generic integrator: it gives
any edge a polarity and any node a kind, which similarity cannot, and the two compose, the embedding
proposing and the decision model judging. Its answers belong in typed fields beside the vector,
derived by a drain, never guessed when pending, attributed as a source and kept local unless an
instance allows otherwise. On this machine the 0.8-billion-parameter local model is not yet good
enough to be the default; the hosted model is, for polarity, kind and reply, where data may leave
the machine.
