Use-Driven Weights that Stay Bounded, Forget, and Never Collapse}
\author{Alberto Serrano-Calva}
\date{November 8, 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
A knowledge store's relations are structural: explicit edges, lexical and semantic similarities.
They say what is connected, not what is used or what its use produced. We design a second kind of
value, a \emph{utility field}: one number per site (node, edge or declared path) that each tick
decays toward a baseline $b$ at a rate $\lambda$ and receives the tick's use clamped to $[-c, c]$,
and that enters ranking as a multiplicative nudge $1 + \eta(u - b)$ of the structural weights with
margin $\theta = \eta c/\lambda$. Six laws, machine-checked in Lean~4 over Mathlib, keep the field
from being steered into a nonsensical state: the box $[b - c/\lambda,\, b + c/\lambda]$ is
invariant and its floor positive (UTL-1); an idle site returns to the baseline within $\varepsilon$
once $\lambda n \ge \log(c/(\lambda\varepsilon))$, so arcs fade and form on closed-form windows
(UTL-2); the nudge keeps the support exactly, no node's stationary mass moves by more than the
ratio $(1+\theta)/(1-\theta)$, the stationary distributions stay within $\theta/(1-\theta)$ in total
variation, and a spectral gap $\gamma$ becomes a gap $(1-\theta)\gamma$ (UTL-3); the field is the
fold of the tick over the use log, and at the baseline its nudge is the identity (UTL-4); the
Laplacian's quadratic
form moves by at most $\theta$ times itself, so by Weyl's inequality every eigenvalue moves by at
most $2\theta d_{\max}$ and the structural order is kept beyond the margin (UTL-5); and the closed
loop in which use depends on the field is a contraction with one stationary state when its gain is
below $\lambda$, with the ceiling a second fixed point at or above it (UTL-6). The controls are
theorems too: reinforcement without decay is unbounded, the two-site mean field of an exponent
separates at one, and the greedy walk localizes. The algebra beneath the laws is attacked on exact
rational shadows beside refuted controls, and a simulator on synthetic graphs shows arcs forming at
a heat spot and fading when the load moves, every bound holding with room, and localization once
the exponent crosses one.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A store that is kept over time accumulates structure (citations, similarities, version chains) and
a theory of its dynamics: the sum over walks is a resolvent, a flow is read through its Hodge
decomposition, the macro regime is the span of the low modes, and the thresholds at which these are
measurable are known (\lean{papers/2026-11-07-graph-dynamics}, \lean{papers/2026-11-07-scale-and-attribution}).
What the structure does not record is use. Which paths a reader actually follows, which results
led to a resolved question, which tokens and vectors are returned often: a store should carry this
as a second kind of value, so that its relationships evolve with their utility, high-value paths
become shortcuts, and ranking is nudged in a favourable direction. The danger is as old as the
idea. A ranking that feeds on its own use is a reinforced random walk, and reinforced walks can
localize: a few nodes visited forever, one edge crossed back and forth, a self-sustaining loop the
structure never asked for.

This article designs the field and proves what keeps it benign. The contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A model (\Cref{sec:model}): a field per site, one tick (decay toward a baseline, then a
  saturated increment), a multiplicative nudge of the structural weights with a margin, arcs as a
  reading of the field, a closed loop, and the models of three controls.
\item Six laws (\Cref{sec:laws}), each a theorem in \lean{formal/Palace/Utility.lean}: boundedness,
  decay with its window, no localization (support, stationary mass, total variation, gap),
  derivation and switch-off, the bounded nudge through Weyl's inequality, and the loop's contraction.
  The controls are theorems of the same file: linear reinforcement without decay is unbounded, the
  exponent's three regimes on the two-site mean field, the greedy walk's confinement.
\item The regime (\Cref{sec:regime}): why the design is a normalized, sublinear reinforcement in the
  sense of the phase transition at exponent one, and where the one remaining risk, the loop's gain,
  is kept small and is the first thing the build measures.
\item Measurements on synthetic graphs (\Cref{sec:eval}): arcs that form and fade on the predicted
  windows, every bound against what was measured, the learning and its forgetting, and the
  controls' localization.
\end{enumerate}
The algebra the theorems rest on is attacked on exact shadows over $\mathbb{Q}$ beside negative
controls; the cited theorems are stated in \lean{formal/Spec/Utility.lean} and judged against the
library by the comparator; the library is replayed through Lean's kernel and five independent
checkers. Nothing in the store is changed: the
field is designed, proved and simulated, and the build order says what a merge licenses.

\section{Background and related work}

\paragraph{Conductance and potential.}
In the resistor-network reading of a weighted graph, a weight is a conductance, a function on the
nodes is a potential, and the effective resistance $R_{\mathrm{eff}}(i,j) = L^+_{ii} + L^+_{jj} -
2L^+_{ij}$ says how easily current passes between two nodes; adding conductance lowers every
effective resistance or leaves it (Rayleigh's law) \cite{doylesnell1984}. A field on the edges is a
flow, and its Hodge decomposition splits it into a gradient, the least-squares ranking of the nodes,
a curl and a harmonic part \cite{jiang2011hodgerank}. Our field multiplies conductances within a
margin, so every effective resistance moves within the reciprocal factors, between
$R_{\mathrm{eff}}/(1+\theta)$ and $R_{\mathrm{eff}}/(1-\theta)$, and its reading on the nodes
is a potential whose gradient is a ranking.

\paragraph{Reinforcement and its pathologies.}
Edge-reinforced random walk with linear reinforcement is, on a finite graph, a mixture of
reversible Markov chains: the walk is partially exchangeable, so by de Finetti's theorem for Markov
chains it is a mixture of chains \cite{diaconis1980definetti,diaconis1988recent}, with an explicit
mixing measure \cite{keane2000errw,rolles2003errw,merkl2005ladder,sabot2015errw}. Reinforcement by
use is then a random environment: benign, nothing collapses, the environment is just not the
structural one. Vertex-reinforced random walk localizes: on $\mathbb{Z}$ it eventually visits five
points forever \cite{pemantle1992vrrw,pemantle1999finite,tarres2004five}, and on bounded-degree
graphs it localizes with positive probability on a complete multipartite subgraph with its boundary
\cite{volkov2001vrrw,benaim2011dynamics}. Strongly reinforced edge walks, with weights $w(n)$ of the
crossing count and $\sum 1/w(n) < \infty$, have an attracting edge crossed forever
\cite{davis1990reinforced,limic2007attracting}. In urns with feedback, where a site's probability is
its weight to the power $\alpha$, the transition is at $\alpha = 1$: above it one site takes all but
finitely many increments, below it the shares equalize
\cite{drinea2002feedback,oliveira2009onset,pemantle2007survey}.

\paragraph{Forgetting as a window.}
Exponential forgetting makes a sequence of increments a geometric moving average
\cite{roberts1959ewma}, whose variance under independent increments equals that of a plain average
over $2/\lambda - 1$ ticks. The sibling theorem SCL-1 says a time average on a graph with gap
$\gamma$ is a stable macro quantity only over a window of many relaxation times, with about
$T\varepsilon\gamma/2$ effective samples.

\paragraph{Popularity against surprise.}
Token frequencies follow a power law in rank \cite{zipf1949} and vocabularies grow as a power of the
text \cite{heaps1978}; the informative weight of a term is its surprise, the inverse document
frequency \cite{sparckjones1972idf,robertson2004idf}, and what a pair adds is their mutual
information against the product of their base rates \cite{church1990mutual,cover2006}. The field
therefore credits value, the log-score advantage of a resolved question (the sibling law GRW-1),
discounted by popularity.

\paragraph{Perturbation and energy.}
Weyl's inequality bounds each eigenvalue's move by the perturbation's norm
\cite{weyl1912,horn2013matrix}; the sibling family SPC-3 proves it in a counted form over
Courant--Fischer's dimension count, and the Laplacian's largest eigenvalue is at most twice the
largest degree \cite{anderson1985eigenvalues}. The walk sum is a partition function over paths with
Boltzmann weights, so a multiplicative nudge is a bounded shift of edge energies and the forgetting
rate plays the part of a temperature.

\section{The model}\label{sec:model}

\begin{definition}[the field and its tick]
Let $\iota$ be the sites and $K$ a linearly ordered field. The saturated increment is
$\sat_c(x) = \max(-c, \min(c, x))$ (\lean{Palace.Utility.sat}). One tick with use $x : \iota \to K$ is
\[
  u'(s) = b + (1-\lambda)\,(u(s) - b) + \sat_c(x(s)) \quad\text{(\lean{step})},
\]
with baseline $b$, rate $\lambda$ and cap $c$. The field after a log of uses is the left fold of the
tick (\lean{evolve}); $n$ ticks with no use is \lean{idle}, with a constant use $r$ is \lean{steady}.
The box is $[b - c/\lambda,\, b + c/\lambda]$ (\lean{floor}, \lean{ceiling}, \lean{InBox}). An arc of
height $a$ is a site with $u(s) \ge b + a$ (\lean{IsArc}). The closed loop with feedback $F$ is
$u \mapsto \mathrm{step}(u, F(u))$ (\lean{loop}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[the nudge]
For a weight matrix $A$ on $V$ and a field $u$ on the pairs, the effective weights are
$A'_{ij} = A_{ij}\,(1 + \eta(u_{ij} - b))$ (\lean{nudge}), with margin
$\theta = \eta\,c/\lambda$ (\lean{margin}). The stationary distribution of the walk on $A$ is
$\pi_i = d_i/\sum_j d_j$ (\lean{statDist}), and $\tv(p, q) = \tfrac12\sum_i\abs{p_i - q_i}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[the controls]
\lean{linearStep} adds the use with no decay and no cap. The two-site mean field of an exponent
$\alpha$ is $(x, y) \mapsto (x + x^\alpha/S,\; y + y^\alpha/S)$ with $S = x^\alpha + y^\alpha$
(\lean{polyaStep}). The greedy walk on the path $0 - 1 - 2$ moves along the heavier incident edge
(the left one on a tie) and adds one to it (\lean{greedyStep}); \lean{greedyEmp} is its empirical
measure over $n$ steps and \lean{path3} the path's unit weights.
\end{definition}

\section{The laws}\label{sec:laws}

Throughout, $0 < \lambda \le 1$, $c \ge 0$, $\eta \ge 0$; the algebraic laws hold over any linearly
ordered field, the window, the spectrum and the metric over $\R$.

\begin{theorem}[UTL-1, boundedness]\label{thm:utl1}
If $u$ is in the box then so is $\mathrm{step}(u, x)$ for every use $x$ (\lean{step\_mem\_box}), and
so is $\mathrm{evolve}(u, \ell)$ for every log $\ell$ (\lean{evolve\_mem\_box}); the floor is positive
when $c < b\lambda$ (\lean{floor\_pos}). The box is the least invariant box: from the baseline, a
site held at the cap climbs past every level below the ceiling (\lean{exists\_steady\_gt}).
Control: with no decay, $n$ uses of $r > 0$ give $u + nr$, past every bound
(\lean{linearStep\_iterate}, \lean{linear\_unbounded}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$(1-\lambda)(u - b) \in [-(1-\lambda)c/\lambda,\,(1-\lambda)c/\lambda]$ and
$\sat_c \in [-c, c]$ add to $[-c/\lambda,\, c/\lambda]$, since
$(1-\lambda)c/\lambda + c = c/\lambda$. The log is an induction on the fold. Sharpness: the closed
form $b + (c/\lambda)(1 - (1-\lambda)^n)$ (\lean{steady\_apply}) exceeds $M < b + c/\lambda$ once
$(1-\lambda)^n < (b + c/\lambda - M)\lambda/c$, which Mathlib's \lean{exists\_pow\_lt\_of\_lt\_one}
gives.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[UTL-2, decay]\label{thm:utl2}
$\mathrm{idle}(u, n)(s) = b + (1-\lambda)^n(u(s) - b)$ (\lean{idle\_apply}); from the box,
$\abs{\mathrm{idle}(u, n)(s) - b} \le (1-\lambda)^n c/\lambda$ (\lean{idle\_sub\_le}); and for
$\varepsilon > 0$, once $\lambda n \ge \log(c/(\lambda\varepsilon))$ the site is within $\varepsilon$ of
the baseline (\lean{idle\_window}). An arc of height $a$ is gone once $(1-\lambda)^n c/\lambda < a$
(\lean{not\_isArc\_idle}) and forms at a site held at the cap from at least the baseline once
$(c/\lambda)(1 - (1-\lambda)^n) \ge a$ (\lean{isArc\_steady}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Induction gives the closed forms. For the window, $1 - \lambda \le e^{-\lambda}$
(Mathlib's \lean{Real.add\_one\_le\_exp}), so $(1-\lambda)^n \le e^{-\lambda n}$
(\lean{one\_sub\_pow\_le\_exp}), and $e^{-\lambda n} \le \lambda\varepsilon/c$ exactly when
$\lambda n \ge \log(c/(\lambda\varepsilon))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[UTL-3, no localization]\label{thm:utl3}
Let $A \ge 0$ with positive volume, $u$ in the box on every pair and $\theta < 1$. Then
$A'_{ij} = 0 \iff A_{ij} = 0$ (\lean{nudge\_eq\_zero\_iff}; the support is equal,
\lean{support\_nudge}); for every node (\lean{le\_statDist\_nudge}, \lean{statDist\_nudge\_le})
\[
  \frac{1-\theta}{1+\theta}\,\pi_i \;\le\; \pi'_i \;\le\; \frac{1+\theta}{1-\theta}\,\pi_i,
\]
and $\tv(\pi', \pi) \le \theta/(1-\theta)$ (\lean{tv\_statDist\_nudge\_le}). If moreover $A$ and $u$
are symmetric and $\gamma$ is a spectral gap of $A$ in the Poincar\'e form of DYN-6, then
$(1-\theta)\gamma$ is a gap of $A'$ (\lean{hasSpectralGapAtLeast\_nudge}), so every nonzero
eigenvalue of $L'$ is at least $(1-\theta)\gamma$ (\lean{le\_eigenvalue\_nudge}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The factor $1 + \eta(u_{ij} - b)$ lies in $[1-\theta, 1+\theta]$ (\lean{nudge\_factor\_mem}), so
$(1-\theta)A_{ij} \le A'_{ij} \le (1+\theta)A_{ij}$ and the same for degrees and the volume; the
ratio bounds follow by dividing, and
$\abs{\pi'_i - \pi_i} \le \frac{2\theta}{1-\theta}\pi_i$ sums to $\theta/(1-\theta)$ since
$\sum_i\pi_i = 1$. The Dirichlet identity $2x^\top L x = \sum_{ij}A_{ij}(x_i - x_j)^2$ (LAP-2)
gives $(1-\theta)\,x^\top Lx \le x^\top L'x$ (\lean{le\_dotProduct\_lap\_nudge}), hence the gap, and
DYN-6's \lean{le\_eigenvalue\_of\_hasSpectralGapAtLeast} reads it on the modes.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[UTL-4, derivation]
$\mathrm{evolve}(u_0, \ell_1 \mathbin{+\!\!+} \ell_2) = \mathrm{evolve}(\mathrm{evolve}(u_0, \ell_1), \ell_2)$
(\lean{evolve\_append}), and $\mathrm{nudge}(\eta, b, A, b) = A$ (\lean{nudge\_baseline}): the field
is a function of its log, and switching it off recovers the structural weights exactly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[UTL-5, the bounded nudge]\label{thm:utl5}
For symmetric $A \ge 0$, a symmetric field in the box and every $x$,
$\abs{x^\top(L' - L)x} \le \theta\,x^\top L x$ (\lean{abs\_dotProduct\_lap\_sub\_le}), and
$x^\top L x \le 2d\,\norm{x}^2$ when every degree is at most $d$
(\lean{dotProduct\_lap\_le\_degree}). Hence, as operators on Euclidean space, $L'$ lies within
$2\theta d$ of $L$ in SPC-3's sense (\lean{near\_lap\_nudge}), and with $N_M(t)$ the number of
eigenvalues of $M$ at most $t$, Weyl's counts give (\lean{count\_lap\_nudge\_sub\_le},
\lean{count\_lap\_nudge\_le\_add})
\[
  N_L(t - 2\theta d) \;\le\; N_{L'}(t) \;\le\; N_L(t + 2\theta d).
\]
Two structural weights with $(1+\theta)A_{kl} < (1-\theta)A_{ij}$ keep their order under every
field state (\lean{nudge\_lt\_nudge\_of\_margin}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The Dirichlet identity termwise with the factor bounds gives both sides of
$(1\pm\theta)x^\top Lx$; $(x_i - x_j)^2 \le 2(x_i^2 + x_j^2)$ and symmetry give the degree bound.
The inner product on $\mathrm{EuclideanSpace}$ is the dot product and $\mathrm{toEuclideanLin}$ is
multiplication by the matrix, so the quadratic-form bound is SPC-3's \lean{Near} hypothesis and its
\lean{count\_sub\_le}, \lean{count\_le\_add} apply. By Courant--Fischer the relative bound also gives
$(1-\theta)\lambda_k \le \lambda_k' \le (1+\theta)\lambda_k$, which the unit tests check and the
Lean states only through the gap.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[UTL-6, no self-reinforcing loop]\label{thm:utl6}
Let $\iota$ be finite and $F$ satisfy $\mathrm{dist}(F u, F v) \le K\,\mathrm{dist}(u, v)$ in the sup
metric, with $0 \le K$. Then $\mathrm{dist}(\mathrm{loop}\,u, \mathrm{loop}\,v) \le (1 - \lambda +
K)\,\mathrm{dist}(u, v)$ (\lean{loop\_dist\_le}); if $K < \lambda$ the loop has exactly one fixed
field (\lean{loop\_existsUnique\_fixedPoint}) and its iterates converge to it at the rate
$1 - \lambda + K$ (\lean{loop\_iterate\_tendsto}). Control: for $F(u) = K(u - b)$ with $K \ge
\lambda$ and $c > 0$, both the baseline and the ceiling are fixed, so uniqueness fails
(\lean{Witness.bistable}, \lean{Witness.not\_unique}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Pointwise, since the clamp is $1$-Lipschitz (\lean{abs\_sat\_sub\_sat\_le}),
\[
  \abs{(1-\lambda)(u_e - v_e) + \sat_c(F u\,e) - \sat_c(F v\,e)} \le
  (1-\lambda)\abs{u_e - v_e} + \abs{F u\,e - F v\,e},
\]
and the sup metric's \lean{dist\_pi\_le\_iff} and \lean{dist\_le\_pi\_dist} finish. With $1 - \lambda + K < 1$,
Mathlib's \lean{ContractingWith.fixedPoint} on the complete space $\iota \to \R$ gives existence,
uniqueness and the a priori rate. At the ceiling, $K(c/\lambda) \ge c$ saturates the increment to $c$
and $b + (1-\lambda)c/\lambda + c = b + c/\lambda$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the exponent decides; the greedy limit localizes]\label{prop:controls}
For $0 < y < x$ and $m \ge 1$, the two-site mean field at exponent $m+1$ increases the ratio $x/y$
(\lean{polya\_ratio\_lt}); at exponent $1$ it holds it (\lean{polya\_ratio\_eq}); at exponent $0$ it
moves it toward one (\lean{polya\_ratio\_gt}). The greedy walk started off the far node with the left
edge at least as heavy never reaches the far node (\lean{greedy\_pos\_ne\_two}), so its empirical
measure gives the far node mass $0$ while the structural walk gives it $1/4$
(\lean{statDist\_path3\_two}), and the total variation between them is at least $1/8$ at every
horizon (\lean{greedy\_tv\_ge}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At exponent $m + 1$, $x'y - xy' = xy(x^m - y^m)/(x^{m+1} + y^{m+1})$, positive for $m \ge 1$ and
zero at $m = 0$; at exponent $0$ each site gains $\tfrac12$, so $x'y - xy' = (y - x)/2 < 0$. The
greedy invariant (position not the far node, left
edge at least as heavy) is kept by each step and gives the confinement by induction.
\end{proof}

\section{The design and its regime}\label{sec:regime}

\paragraph{Sites, uses, value.}
A site is a node, an edge of a relation, or a declared path a sentence walked. A use is a record
(site, tick, principal, kind, value, witness) in an append-only log beside the ledger; the field is
the fold of the tick over the log (UTL-4), so it is rebuilt from the log and every value traces to
its uses, and it never writes authored content, labels or relations. The increment a use adds is
$\mathrm{value}\cdot\mathrm{surprise}$: value $1$ for a resolved question whose arm read the site with
a positive log-score advantage over the reference arm (GRW-1's currency, which adds across
questions), $1$ for a citation followed, a declared fraction for a retrieval that resolved
nothing, $-1$ for a resolved question with a negative advantage; surprise $1 - h(s)$ with $h$ the
site's popularity histogram over the window. Both are bounded, so the cap is the most a tick adds.
Popularity histograms of tokens and vectors are fields with $\eta = 0$, windowed counts that give the
base rates and never nudge a ranking directly.

\paragraph{The nudge and arcs.}
Every ranking verb runs on $A' = A \circ (1 + \eta(u - b))$ where it ran on $A$; the support and
the components are $A$'s (UTL-3), the degrees within the factors $1 \pm \theta$ of $A$'s, and $A'$
is symmetric and nonnegative, so the laws the verbs already satisfy keep their hypotheses. An arc is a declared path with $u \ge b + a$: it forms at a heat spot and fades on the
window of \Cref{thm:utl2}; it materializes the path's walk sum for cheap reading and adds no edge.
An ablation arm with $\eta = 0$ is the structural system exactly (UTL-4), so the field is a source
whose contribution the attribution loop measures.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{One tick of the field.}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require the use log's records for the tick; the field $u$ in its box; $b, \lambda, c, \eta, a$
\Ensure $u'$ in the box; the effective weights $A'$; the arc list
\State $x \gets 0$
\For{each record $(s, \mathrm{kind}, \mathrm{value}, \mathrm{witness})$}
  \State $x(s) \gets x(s) + \mathrm{value}\cdot(1 - h(s))$ \Comment{value against popularity}
\EndFor
\State $u' \gets b + (1-\lambda)(u - b) + \sat_c(x)$ \Comment{UTL-1 keeps the box; UTL-2 the window}
\State $A' \gets A \circ (1 + \eta(u' - b))$ \Comment{within the margin $\theta$; UTL-3, UTL-5}
\State arcs $\gets \{P : u'(P) \ge b + a\}$, their walk sums cached
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{The regime.}
\Cref{tab:regime} sets the design against the reinforced walks of the literature. The design is
sublinear and normalized in the exponent's terms: an increment never grows with the site's own
weight, the field is a windowed average rather than a count, and the walk's weights are the
structural ones within a margin. Its stationary law is within $\theta/(1-\theta)$ of the
structural one at every time (UTL-3), where linear reinforcement drifts to a random environment and
superlinear reinforcement localizes. The one way out of the regime is the loop: if the nudge raised
a site's use enough to raise its nudge further with gain $K \ge \lambda$, the hot state would
sustain itself (\Cref{thm:utl6}'s control). The design keeps the gain small, since a nudge moves
each weight only within the margin and the increment counts value, which the ranking does not
produce; whether a real load's gain lies below the rate is a measurement, the first the build
makes.

\paragraph{The build order.}
Nothing is built by this article. A merge licenses four units, each measured on the corpus
instance (its own stores, its own ledger) before anything runs on the default instance, which keeps
$\eta = 0$ until the measurements say otherwise: UF-1 the use log, the tick and the histograms, no
ranking reading them; UF-2 the nudge behind a switch, with the $\eta = 0$ arm registered in the
ledger; UF-3 arcs and their cached walk sums; UF-4 the value increment wired to the ledger's
resolutions and followed citations. Each unit property-tests its transcription against the Lean
statements, and the ledger's rows move their anchors from the simulator to the store's code as it
lands.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

\paragraph{What is proved.}
\Cref{thm:utl1,thm:utl2,thm:utl3,thm:utl5,thm:utl6} and \Cref{prop:controls} are machine-checked in
\lean{formal/Palace/Utility.lean} over \lean{formal/Palace/Utility/Defs.lean} on Lean v4.36.0-rc2 and
the Mathlib it pins \cite{moura2021lean4,mathlib2020}, with the sorry budget at zero and the axioms
of all 4{,}730 library declarations audited (\lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice},
\lean{Quot.sound} only). Twenty statements are restated in \lean{formal/Spec/Utility.lean} and
judged identical to the library's, 306 statements in all; the library's export, 55{,}608
declarations from \lean{Init} up, is replayed by Lean's kernel and by \lean{leanchecker-paranoid},
\lean{lean4lean}, \lean{nanoda}, \lean{con-leche} and \lean{con-ron}.

\paragraph{The attacks.}
The definitions are polymorphic in the field, so the attacks run them over $\mathbb{Q}$ as they are
(\lean{formal/PalaceTest/Utility.lean}, seeds 402 to 424, 500 draws each): fields on four sites
from lists of integers, a baseline $1 + b$, a rate $1/(1+l)$, a cap $c/4$ from naturals, uses of any
size and sign; symmetric nonnegative weights on four nodes with a symmetric field on the edges and a
margin $t/(t+1)$; the loop with a linear gain; the mean field at exponents $0$, $1$, $2$; the greedy
walk for forty steps. The box survived 500 under one tick and 500 under a log; the idle closed form
500; the arc forming 500 and fading 325 (175 draws discarded before the window elapsed); the support
500; the total-variation bound 490 (10 edgeless draws discarded); the quadratic-form and degree
bounds 500 each; the loop's contraction 500; the three exponents 500, as one conjunction; the greedy
confinement 500. The window, the spectrum and the fixed point do not run, so they are proved and
not attacked, and UTL-4 is proved by structure. The controls, each refuted: no decay leaves the box
within 11 instances; the box with ceiling $b + c$ within 14; a residual use holds the field off the
baseline within 6; the additive nudge creates an edge within 40; a field twice the box's height
breaks the total-variation bound within 119 and the form bound within 38; the largest degree alone
does not bound the form within 12; an arc left alone fades, a gain above the rate fixes the
ceiling, exponent 2 moves the ratio, and the greedy walk leaves the structural law, each on the
first instance.

\section{Limitations}

The total-variation constant $\theta/(1-\theta)$ is proved and not tight; the per-node ratio bound
is sharper and the simulation sits far inside both, because a load heats a few paths while the
bounds are for the worst field in the box. The reinforced-walk theorems are cited, not formalized:
what is proved is deterministic, the stationary law under any field state and the mean field of the
exponent, and the greedy walk stands in for the attracting edge. UTL-6 bounds the loop for a
declared gain; the gain of a real load is a measurement the corpus instance makes, and the design's
claim that it lies below the rate is a hypothesis with a named falsifier. The simulator is
synthetic: it says the laws hold and the windows are as predicted, not where a real corpus sits
between the worst field and this one. The learning it shows (one answer of four lifted to first in
the first phase, two in the third) is not explained by the laws, which bound how far each weight
moves and not which of two walk sums is the larger; the run does not separate why the others stayed
second.

\section{Future work}

Build UF-1 to UF-4 on the corpus instance in order, each unit property-tested against the Lean
statements; measure the loop's gain on the use log and the relaxation time that sets $\lambda$;
register the $\eta = 0$ arm and read the field's advantage at the ledger's bar; compare the
value-weighted increment with the popularity-weighted one. Formalize the relative Weyl bound
$(1 \pm \theta)\lambda_k$ through Courant--Fischer rather than through the gap alone, and the
sharp total-variation constant. Extend the model to fields on the vector plane's atoms, whose
reading is a cosine rather than a walk.

\section{Conclusion}

A store can carry a second kind of value above its structure, a field of use and realized value,
and what keeps it bounded is small: the decay, the cap and the margin. The decay and the cap
make the field a windowed average in a box; the margin makes the nudge a bounded perturbation of
the structural weights, whose stationary law, spectrum and order move only within declared factors
and return exactly when the field is removed; and the loop by which use feeds on ranking is a
contraction when its gain is below the rate, with the self-reinforcing state appearing exactly at
the rate as the control. Each statement is a theorem, the algebra beneath them is attacked beside
refuted controls, the arcs form and fade on the predicted windows, and the regime the design sits
in is the benign one, with its single exit named and the measurement that would show it the
build's first.
