\begin{abstract}
A design for growing a private knowledge store toward a hundred gigabytes of open text names three
stages and, for the first, a table of measurements with the observations that would falsify each
expectation. We report the first stage. Five open sources (Wikipedia, OpenAlex, arXiv, Stack Exchange and Project Gutenberg; Hacker News is being added)  were read by edge processes into a named instance whose
every namespace is derived from its id and shown, by the store's own check over real paths, to share
nothing with the live instance. Items land through the store's ingress in batches whose stages run
across the batch with each item's key written last, a form of the ingress's convergence laws that a
crash test checks at four cut points. The landing is two-phase: every item lands first with its label,
its explicit relations and a BM25 index, which needs no model; its chunk vectors are derived from the
raw store afterwards and are pending until then. On the structure alone: the citation graph's 6-core
and 10-core spectral gaps settle by $5\times10^4$ nodes and its Hodge split a decade earlier; the
hyperlink graph's gap settles near $10^4$; reply, answer and version relations form forests with no
core. Ninety-nine pairs of communities across sources are joined more often than a degree-preserving
null joins them (a false-positive bar of 46), and communities rise together within a source
($\rho_w = 0.12$) but across sources only at the level of a circular-shift null ($\rho_b = 0.09$),
for an effective count of independent signals near 9. Lexical recall at ten falls as the corpus grows
in seven of nine question families, by up to 0.36; restricting the search to the answer's source
removes most of the fall caused by other sources but not the fall caused by the source's own growth.
A sentence of the store's query algebra over the observed stratum loads every item of it and costs
6.8\,s and 3.7\,GB at ten thousand items, so at a million chunks it is out of reach as built.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A private store of a few dozen notes sits orders of magnitude below the sizes at which the macro
quantities of a graph stop fluctuating, so its macro behaviour cannot be studied in place. The
corpus-instance design \citep{serranocalva2026palace} answers with a separate, named instance of the
same system, grown in three stages of 1, 10 and 100 gigabytes of open text, each stage with a table of
measurements and falsifiers. This paper is the first stage: what was built so that it could run, what
was ingested, and every row of its table.

Two pieces of theory frame the measurements. Information never hurts an ideal reasoner, but a reader
that sees only the top $k$ of a ranked corpus loses recall as the corpus grows (crowding), and the
remedy the system offers is a scope sized by the question. And the number of independent signals a
set of sources gives is capped by their correlation, so varied sources help exactly to the extent
that they are disparate. Stage 1 asks whether the sources connect, whether crowding is real at a
million chunks, and what the system costs at that size.

The contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item a named instance with its isolation proved by the store's own check, and a runner that is its
  only writer (\cref{sec:instance});
\item a bulk path through the ingress that lands a batch stage by stage with each key last, and a
  two-phase landing in which structure lands first and vectors are a derived layer added after,
  both with their convergence argued and tested (\cref{sec:theory,sec:impl});
\item 0.90\,GB of text from five sources, 1.2 million chunks, with Hacker News being added (\cref{sec:data});
\item the structural measurements of the stage-1 table: macro quantities per relation with bootstrap
  widths, bridges and shared rises against nulls, the lexical retrieval battery with crowding and
  attribution, and cost against $N$ (\cref{sec:eval});
\item the vector-phase measurements: the battery three ways, the embedder's attribution as a source,
  semantic bridges and the codes written for stage 2 (\cref{sec:vectors}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
The store keeps its items in a raw content-addressed store, a catalog whose digest is the idempotence
key of a landing, and a shared plane of chunk vectors read through typed clearances. Its ingress lands
requests written in a small language under one writer, with laws proved in Lean: a re-ingest of held
content embeds nothing (ING-5), a run interrupted after any step converges when run again provided the
key is written last (ING-7), and steps of commands on distinct identities commute, so any interleaving
cut anywhere converges under re-run (ING-9, ING-11). The scale study measured, on one citation graph,
where the spectral gap $\gamma$ of the normalized Laplacian and the Hodge split \citep{jiang2011hodgerank}
of the citation flow stop fluctuating under resampling: on the 6-core and 10-core
\citep{seidman1983cores} once they hold one to two thousand nodes with minimum degree near $\ln N$,
consistent with spectral concentration for random graphs \citep{chung2011spectra}, and the Hodge split
one to two decades earlier. The attribution note proves that the Shapley value \citep{shapley1953value}
is the unique efficient, symmetric, null-player and additive attribution, and that it disagrees with
leave-one-out exactly when sources are redundant.

\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}
\begin{lemma}[crowding]
Let a scorer totally order a corpus and $R_k(C)$ be the $k$ best items of $C$. If $C\subseteq C'$
then $R_k(C')\cap C\subseteq R_k(C)$, so for a relevant $r\in C$, $[r\in R_k(C')]\le[r\in R_k(C)]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An item of $C$ among the $k$ best of $C'$ is beaten by fewer than $k$ items of $C'$, hence by fewer
than $k$ items of $C$.
\end{proof}
So recall at $k$ is nonincreasing along nested corpora that keep the relevant items, whatever is
added; the measurement is the rate. A scope is a restriction to a set $\sigma\ni r$; the lemma applies
inside it, so a scope helps by the items it excludes and still crowds as $\sigma$ itself grows.

\begin{proposition}[effective count \citep{kish1965}]
With $D$ groups of $m$ unit-variance signals, correlation $\rho_w$ within a group and $\rho_b$ across,
the variance of their mean is $(1+(m-1)\rho_w+m(D-1)\rho_b)/(Dm)$, so the effective number of
independent signals is $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = Dm/(1+(m-1)\rho_w+m(D-1)\rho_b)$, which tends to
$D/(\rho_w+(D-1)\rho_b)$ as $m\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[the staged batch converges]
Let a batch be stores on pairwise distinct paths, each naming its path's own identity, and let a
store's steps be its raw object, its plane rows, its attestation and its key, in that order. A
landing that runs each stage over the whole batch before the next is a permutation of the batch's
steps that keeps each store's own order; cut at any point, every store has run a prefix of its own
steps. By ING-11 the batch run again lands the uninterrupted batch.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The stages are ordered and each store's steps fall one to a stage, so a cut leaves store $i$ at the
prefix of its steps up to the cut's stage. Distinct paths and own identities make the stores commands
on distinct identities, which is ING-11's hypothesis (\lean{Palace.Ingress.batch\_after\_prefixes}).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[two phases end where one ends]
Let phase 1 land each store's raw object, attestation and key and no plane rows, and phase 2 derive
each keyed item's plane rows from its raw bytes with the same chunker and embedder. If the embedder is
deterministic, the state after both phases equals the state of one landing.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The raw object, the attestation and the key are written by phase 1 exactly as one landing writes them
(the plan, the rules and the order are the same). The plane rows of a version are a function of its
bytes, the chunker and the embedder; phase 2 computes them from the bytes the raw store holds under
the key's digest and lands them through the same plane landing, which is idempotent by reconciliation.
\end{proof}
Between the phases an item has a key and no rows. A reader that needs a vector must therefore read
the plane, not the key, and report the item pending; the store's query universe already keeps nodes
without vectors apart by a known mask.

\section{The instance and its isolation}\label{sec:instance}
The instance's id is \code{corpus}; its root, data, spools, lock, levers, streams and results lie
under \code{instances/corpus/} of the worktree, and its Keychain items and launchd labels are derived
from the id. Before anything was fetched, the store's check (\code{shared} over \code{namespaces\_of},
every location compared by real path, one nested in another counted as shared) ran against the
worktree's default instance, the bench instance declared beside it, and the live default instance of
the main checkout, whose two configuration overlays were read and nothing else. All three lists were
empty. As a control, a written path that is a link into the live instance's data directory was found
shared at all sixteen of the live instance's written locations, so the check has power. The runner
repeats the proof before it writes and takes the instance's own supervisor lock; it ran as processes
started and stopped by hand.

\section{Implementation}\label{sec:impl}
\paragraph{Streams and the batch argument.}
An edge reader appends items to a stream: part files of one JSON item a line, a part named in a
manifest only once whole. The ingress gains one reading-function argument, \code{items(h, batch: b)},
the next $b$ items past the stream's cursor; the cursor moves only after the plan that stored every
item of the batch has landed. The library script is six lines:
\begin{verbatim}
param name, batch = 2000
for it in items(source("corpus/" + name), batch: batch) {
    store it.content in "observed" at "corpus/" + name + "/" + it.id
}
\end{verbatim}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{The staged batch landing (\code{Writer.land\_batch})}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $A \gets$ the stores the rules admit and the UNCHANGED check does not skip
\For{$s\in A$} write $s$'s raw object and derive its chunks \EndFor
\If{vectors} embed the batch's atoms the plane lacks; land every fiber in one transaction \EndIf
\State read the ledger's producers of the batch's digests once
\For{$s\in A$} attest $s$ unless attested; record $s$'s key \Comment{committed together, keys last} \EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{What the bulk path changed in the stores.}
Three per-item costs grew with the store and would have dominated a million chunks: a LanceDB write
and a cache rewrite per landing (now one write a batch, the new atoms inserted with their cache
entries), a pass over the whole attestation ledger in Python per landing, twice (now one SQL pass a
batch), and a commit per key and per attestation (now one commit for each, the attestations before the
keys). The lander's own share fell from 138\,s to under 0.1\,s per batch of 1,753 items.

\paragraph{The lexical index.}
Each landed chunk is a unit; its text is lower-cased and cut into runs of letters and digits; the
index stores the BM25 weight \citep{robertson2009bm25}
$w(t,d)=\mathrm{idf}(t)\,\frac{tf\,(k_1+1)}{tf+k_1(1-b+b\,|d|/\overline{|d|})}$ with $k_1=1.2$,
$b=0.75$ in one sparse matrix, and a query's score is the sum of its terms' columns. Lexical and vector
rankings are combined by reciprocal rank fusion \citep{cormack2009rrf}, $\sum_r 1/(60+\mathrm{rank}_r)$.

\paragraph{The yield policy.}
The embedder is shared with the live instance and with the chat model's Metal device. The runner sends
four requests of 32 texts in flight, starts none while the chat model's process spends CPU (and for
twenty seconds after), stops at once on an error naming Metal or memory, and keeps 25\,GB of disk free.

\section{Evaluation: structure}\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Bridges}
The graph at the item grain joins every explicit relation and every explicit bridge (citations by DOI
and arXiv id, links, the twins). The null is the configuration model \citep{molloy1995critical}: the
edges rewired by matching their endpoints' stubs at random, every degree kept. A pair of communities
of different sources is a bridge when more edges join it than the null's mean plus three of its
standard deviations; the same count made on a null draw scored against the other draws gives the
false positives. Communities are OpenAlex topics, arXiv primary categories, a question's first tag,
a book's Library of Congress class, a thread's link host, and Louvain communities

\subsection{Attribution over sources}
$v(S)$ is the share of title questions answered (the item or its twin in the first ten) with only the
sources in $S$ present. Over all title questions the lexical arm's Shapley values are close to its
leave-one-out values except for arXiv (0.16 against 0.08), whose records OpenAlex also holds. On the
questions whose answer has a twin, OpenAlex and arXiv each carry about half (Shapley 0.49 and 0.50) and
neither is necessary (leave-one-out 0.00 and $-0.01$, Holm \citep{holm1979simple} $p=1$): the
redundancy witness of SCL-3 appears on this corpus.

\section{Evaluation: vectors}\label{sec:vectors}
The vector phase derives every landed item's chunk vectors from the raw store under the yield policy (four requests of 32 texts, about 35 chunks a second). It is in progress; until an item's rows land, every query that needs its vector reports it pending. This section reports, when the phase completes, the retrieval battery three ways (lexical, semantic, and both by reciprocal rank fusion), the embedder's attribution as a player of its own in the two-player game with the lexical index, the semantic bridges, the BEIR control \citep{thakur2021beir}, and the vector codes written beside the float16 vectors of EmbeddingGemma \citep{vera2025embeddinggemma} for stage 2.

\section{Limitations}
The sample is one corpus at one scale; the curves against $N$ are read within it by cuts and nested
subsets, not across stages. OpenAlex is short of its share. arXiv came through arXiv's query API
because the OAI-PMH endpoint did not answer on the day; its version chains have two dated ends. The
Hodge split of a dense graph is measured on a node subsample. Wikipedia links resolve by exact title,
so a link through a redirect is lost. The rises read a window in which Gutenberg releases are sparse.

\section{Future work}
Stage 2 compares the codes written here against exact answers at ten gigabytes; the universe of the
query algebra needs a support bounded by the question before the observed stratum can grow; and a scope
finer than a source (a community or a core) is the next candidate remedy for crowding.

\section{Conclusion}
The instance is isolated, its bulk path converges, and its structure measured before any vector:
the sources connect beyond a degree-preserving null through specific community pairs, rise together
only within a source, and crowd lexical recall as the corpus grows, which a source scope corrects for
the crowding other sources cause and not for a source's own growth.
